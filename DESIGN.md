# Cardle — Design Document (v2, decisions locked)

Status: **finalized for implementation planning.** v1's open questions are resolved below (§9). This supersedes v1's leaderboard/unlimited-mode/backend/art sections.

---

## 1. Core Critique — where the original brief fought its own goals

(Unchanged from v1 — still the reasoning behind everything below.)

1. **Deal luck isn't evened out anywhere by raw score alone.** Two equally skilled players can post wildly different scores purely from the deal. Fixed by Decision Rating (§3) as a *secondary* skill-over-time metric, while raw score stays primary and exciting (per owner decision, §9.1).
2. **`+5/Ace`, `+2/Face Card` reward the deal, not the decision.** Replaced with decision-flavored bonuses (§3c).
3. ~~Too many always-on bonus categories dilutes the core rule. Kept the bonus list short.~~ **Reversed by owner decision (§9.8):** the risk was cognitive load *during play*, not quantity of bonuses *after the fact*. Since bonuses are only revealed post-hoc in the animated results list (§10), not tracked live while deciding what to discard, a long RNGDLE-style pile of small named bonuses adds excitement without adding in-the-moment complexity. Decision Rating (§3d) is computed from base hand score alone, so no amount of extra bonus categories dilutes the skill signal — see §3f.
4. **"Same Color" as a flat bonus was redundant with Flush.** Cut.
5. **One shot per day + real variance is a harder sell than Wordle's all-skill guesses.** Solved structurally: exclusivity (no unlimited mode, §9.2) makes the one shot feel like an event rather than a compromise, and Decision Rating means a bad deal still lets you feel good about how you played it.
6. ~~Unlimited mode needs a firewall from Daily.~~ **Resolved by removing unlimited mode entirely (§9.2).**
7. **Modifiers that redefine "best hand" need a re-mapped score table shown explicitly that day.** Still true, unchanged.

## 2. Core Loop

1. Player gets 5 cards, once per day. Seed = hash of the date — identical deal for every player that day. **The deal itself is a reveal, not instant** (owner request, §3k): the page opens on a face-down "draw screen," then flips each card face-up left to right using the same animation as a discard's replacement reveal, rarity-aware timing included.
2. Player discards 0–3 cards by default; **some modifiers extend this to 4 or up to 5** (§4, §9.3).
3. Replacements drawn from the remaining seeded deck.
4. Final hand scored: **Hand Score + Flavor Bonuses + Suit Synergy Bonus (§3e) + Skill Bonuses + Modifier Bonus**.
5. Player sees: Score, Decision Rating, Poker Story (themed), emoji share card.
6. **No unlimited/practice mode.** Cardle is a one-a-day activity, full stop — this is deliberate (§9.2), not a v1 scope cut. If a "just one more" itch needs somewhere to go, it goes into tomorrow, not into a practice queue.

## 3. Scoring System

### 3a. Hand Score (base) — odds-proportional (owner request, §3s), rank-scaled within each category (§3u)

Royal Flush 54,800,000 · Straight Flush 6,080,000 · Four of a Kind 351,000 · Full House 58,700 · Flush 42,900 · Straight 21,600 · Three of a Kind 4,000 · Two Pair 1,780 · Pair 200 · High Card 0 are each category's AVERAGE value, scored proportional to real 5-card poker odds and anchored at Pair = 200 (§3s). Every category except High Card and Royal Flush now varies around that average by its decisive rank (§3u) — e.g. a Pair of Aces (278) scores more than a Pair of 2s (122) — so these numbers are reference points, not the literal score every hand of that category gets. Prior flat values, for history: 5000/4000/3250/2500/1750/1500/900/500/200/0 (§3l), originally 1000/800/650/500/350/300/180/100/40/0.

### 3b. Flavor Bonuses (small, capped, luck-flavored — deliberately minor)

+25 per Ace (max +100), +10 per Face Card (max +50). **Cap total at +125** so they season, not swing, the score.

### 3c. Skill Bonuses (renamed/finalized per owner's picks — these reward the decision, not the shuffle)

- ⭐ **Perfect Keep** — held all five cards and still landed an excellent hand.
- 🎯 **Optimal Discard** — your discard matched the mathematically best play (computed via EV simulation over the remaining deck).
- 🔥 **Long Shot** — improved by three or more hand ranks after drawing.
- 💎 **Clean Finish** — every card in the final hand contributes; no dead weight.
- 🧠 **Modifier Master** — solved the day's modifier optimally.
- **Daily Modifier Completed** — +50 flat, for meeting the modifier's basic objective (Modifier Master is the *optimal*-play version of this, worth more).

### 3d. Decision Rating

`Decision Rating = your score ÷ max score achievable from your exact starting hand with optimal play, as a %.`

Worked example (per owner): today's hand K♠ K♥ 8♦ 5♣ 2♠, optimal EV = 510.
- Player A scores 505 → 99% Decision Rating
- Player B scores 470 → 92%
- Player C scores 320 → 63%

This is the number that separates "got lucky" from "played well," and feeds Skill Rating (§8) rather than the daily leaderboard directly.

### 3e. Suit Synergy Bonus (new)

A base, always-on mechanic — not to be confused with the "Suit Bonus" *daily modifier* in §4, which randomly features one suit for extra points on a given day. Suit Synergy applies every day, to whichever suit you end up most concentrated in.

Whichever suit has the most cards in your **final hand** (2 or more) scores:
- **Number Bonus** — 5× the sum of the ranks of just those matching cards (e.g. 2♥ + 3♥ = (2+3)×5 = 25).
- **Group Bonus** — a flat bonus scaled to how many cards share the suit: 2 cards +40, 3 cards +80, 4 cards +140, 5 cards (a flush) +200.

Worked example: 2♥ + 3♥ in your final hand scores 25 (numbers) + 40 (group) = **65 points**.

Kept modest relative to the hand-rank table (max 5000, §3l) so it never reorders which poker hand wins — a maximally-suited High Card tops out well under Three of a Kind (900). It *does* stack with Flush/Straight Flush/Royal Flush (which are already 5-of-a-suit), adding up to +200 on top — this widens the gap between Flush and the hand below it without threatening hands above it.

Worth knowing for calibration: with 5 cards drawn from 4 suits, the pigeonhole principle guarantees *some* suit has 2+ cards every single hand — so this bonus (at least the size-2 tier) fires on effectively every run, not just some. That's a feature, not a bug: no hand ever feels totally bonus-less on the suit front, and the bonus still scales up meaningfully the more concentrated your suits get.

### 3f. Extra Bonuses — the RNGDLE-style stacking layer

Per owner direction (§9.8): the game should feel like RNGDLE, where a pile of small named "somethings happened" bonuses keeps adding up rather than the score coming from one or two big levers. Built as an open-ended **registry** (`src/core/bonus-registry.js`) rather than a fixed list — each entry is an independent, self-contained check against the final hand (and, for a few, the discard), so new ones can be appended indefinitely without touching scoring.js. Any number can fire on the same hand and they all stack.

Individually small (30–90 pts, scaled ×5 alongside the rest of the point system — §3l) so no single one reorders which poker hand wins — the excitement is in *how many* land at once, not any one being huge. Current catalog:

| Bonus | Condition | Points |
|---|---|---|
| 🌈 Rainbow | All 4 suits appear in the final hand | 50 |
| ⚫ Monochrome | Every card is the same color | 75 |
| 🃏 Royal Trio | Hand contains a J, Q, and K | 75 |
| 🌱 Small Stack | Every card is rank 6 or lower | 60 |
| 🧮 Prime Cut | Every rank is prime (2,3,5,7,11,13) | 90 |
| 🔢 Big Numbers | Rank sum ≥ 50 | 50 |
| 🐣 Baby Steps | Rank sum ≤ 30 | 50 |
| 🦄 Bookends | Lowest + highest rank = 16 | 40 |
| 🪜 Three Straight | Longest run of consecutive ranks in the hand is exactly 3 (e.g. 8, 9, 10) | 30 |
| 🌉 Near Miss | Longest run of consecutive ranks in the hand is exactly 4 | 60 |
| 🧹 No Waste | Every discarded card was rank 8 or lower | 40 |
| 🎲 Full Send | Discarded the maximum allowed that day | 30 |
| 🌟 Comeback Kid | Opening hand was High Card, final hand scores | 30 |
| 🎰 Jackpot Digits | Rank sum is a multiple of 10 | 30 |
| 💯 Lucky 21 | Rank sum is exactly 21 | 75 |
| ✋ Steady Hand | Discarded zero cards, regardless of hand quality | 40 |

Steady Hand and ⭐ Perfect Keep (§3c) are deliberately two separate bonuses covering the same action at different bars: Perfect Keep requires holding pat *and* landing an excellent hand (Three of a Kind+); Steady Hand just requires holding pat, full stop, so there's always some reward for trusting a weak-looking deal — they stack when both apply.

Three Straight and Near Miss are two tiers of the same underlying signal (longest run of consecutive ranks in the final hand) and are mutually exclusive by construction — a hand's longest run is a single number, so it can only ever be exactly 3 or exactly 4, never both. A genuine 5-card straight already has a run of 5, so neither tier fires on it — that hand is rewarded by the base hand-rank score instead.

Deliberately not capped as a group (unlike Flavor Bonuses, §3b) — a hand that trips several of these at once (rare) is meant to feel like a jackpot moment. Max theoretical stacking is still small relative to the hand-rank table (roughly a thousand points at most, against a 5000-point Royal Flush), so it can't invert hand-rank ordering, only add flavor and swing the *raw score* leaderboard (§8, Tab 1) — which is exactly the leaderboard this is meant to make exciting. It has zero effect on Decision Rating (§3d), which is computed from base hand score alone — so the skill leaderboard (§8, Tab 2) stays completely unaffected by how many extra bonuses pile up.

### 3g. Luck / Skill / Risk Meters ✅ built (owner request)

Three 0–100% dials shown after Decision Rating, summarizing the run from three different angles (`src/core/meters.js`). Deliberately three separate lenses on the same run, not three independent random numbers:

- **🎯 Skill** — directly reuses Decision Rating (§3d), capped at 100%. No new concept; just surfaced as a dial instead of only a percentage line.
- **🍀 Luck** — blends two things: *deal quality* (how strong the starting hand's ceiling was, from the EV solver's `bestEV`) and *draw fortune* (did the specific draw run hot or cold relative to its own average outcome). Deliberately independent of whether the discard choice itself was smart — that's Skill's job. A player who played perfectly can still register low Luck if the deck simply didn't cooperate.
- **🎲 Risk** — 0% for holding pat (nothing was gambled); otherwise scales with how many cards were let go, boosted by how much value was already sitting in the original hand (walking away from a decent hand to chase something else reads riskier than discarding from nothing).

All three are pure functions of data already computed for scoring/EV — no new inputs needed. Calibration constants (`DEAL_QUALITY_CEILING`, `DRAW_FORTUNE_SCALE`, risk weights) are tuned against realistic score ranges, not derived from first principles — expect to retune once more real hands are played.

### 3h. Card Rarity & the Wild Joker ✅ built (owner request, rebalanced five times — see §3l/§3m/§3n/§3p for the later passes)

Every card dealt or drawn (`src/core/deck.js` `dealHand`) independently rolls a rarity tier, deterministically from the day's seed so it's fair (same day = same rarities for everyone) — single source of truth in `src/core/rarity.js`. Drop chances are unchanged since launch; **points were rebalanced four times**: first from a flat number per tier to `basePoints + perRankPoints × rank`; then a ×5 whole-system rescale that also added a per-tier `multiplier` (§3l); then an odds-calibration pass (§3m) that recomputed every tier's actual values from real poker-hand-vs-rarity probability; then a follow-up (§3n) that gave Joker its own escalating sub-tiers and then *simplified that back down* to reuse Bronze/Silver/Gold directly, per direct owner feedback both times:

| Tier | Chance per card | Points formula | Range (rank 2 → Ace) | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🥉 Bronze | 7% | 150 + 12×rank | 174 – 318 | ×1.2 |
| 🥈 Silver | 3% | 210 + 18×rank | 246 – 462 | ×1.5 |
| 🥇 Gold | 1% | 300 + 25×rank | 350 – 650 | ×2.5 |
| 🃏 Joker | 0.2% | flat, from whichever of Bronze/Silver/Gold/Diamond it rolls | 150 / 210 / 300 / 850 | ×1.2 / ×1.5 / ×2.5 / ×15 |
| 💎 Diamond | 0.1% | 850 + 70×rank | 990 – 1830 | ×15 |

Diamond (§3p) is the rarest tier of all — rarer than Joker itself — added later the same session per owner request ("added rarities higher than gold... very rare, insanely high multiplier"). Since Joker's flavor roll is "whichever non-joker RARITIES tier," Diamond automatically became a possible (vanishingly rare) Joker flavor too, no separate wiring needed.

Bronze/Silver/Gold are pure bonus flourishes — they never change what poker hand you have, just add points (`rarityBonus()` in `scoring.js`, one line per rare card in the breakdown, stacking like the extra-bonus registry) and, when genuinely part of the winning combination, multiply the whole run's total (`rarityMultiplier()`, §3n). **Joker is different: it's genuinely wild.** `hand-evaluator.js`'s `evaluateHand()` detects a joker-rarity card and tries all 52 possible rank/suit substitutions in its place, keeping whichever scores highest — e.g. four broadway cards + a joker correctly resolves to a Royal Flush, not just "a flush" (owner's own worked example, confirming this pre-existing behavior: "if you had a 10 J Q K all hearts and then you pulled a joker, the joker would act like a Ace of hearts completing the royal flush"). This required refactoring the hand-ranking logic into an internal `evaluateFixedHand()` plus a wrapper, but the non-joker path is byte-for-byte unchanged (verified: all pre-existing hand-evaluator tests pass untouched) — the wild logic is purely additive.

**Joker's own flavor (owner request, final form: "i want the joker to just have the same rarities as the normal cards, bronze, silver and gold").** An earlier pass gave Joker its own escalating Common/Rare/Epic/Legendary ladder (up to ×20) — the owner asked for it to be simplified back down to reuse the exact same tiers as every other card, accepting a lower per-Joker ceiling in exchange for consistency (the "insane points" ceiling comes from §3n's whole-hand multiply instead, plus multiple rare cards stacking, plus — added later the same session — Diamond, §3p). Whenever a card rolls the outer `joker` tier, `dealHand()` immediately rolls a *second* tier from every non-joker `RARITIES` entry (`jokerTierForRoll()`) — chances renormalized to their relative proportions (70:30:10:1), so "most Jokers are bronze-flavored, a Diamond Joker is vanishingly rare" reads the same way rarity does everywhere else. Points and multiplier both come from whichever tier it landed on — a Gold Joker is worth exactly what a plain Gold card is worth, flat regardless of its own underlying rank (the rank is an implementation detail of the wild search, not something the player experiences directly). Visually: the Joker's border/glow color matches its flavor (`card-view.js` / `.card--joker-tier-bronze/silver/gold/diamond` in `styles.css`, reusing the exact same colors as the plain cards), and its on-card label reads e.g. "GOLD JOKER" instead of a generic "JOKER".

Because `ev-solver.js` calls `evaluateHand()` extensively, a joker present in the hand makes the full EV solve ~15x more expensive (52x cost on the subset of combinations that include it) — empirically ~1.9s worst-case versus ~130ms normally (measured and regression-tested in `tests/ev-solver.test.js`). Deliberately **not** engineered away: joker hands are rare (~1% of days), and the pause is reframed as intentional drama rather than a performance bug to eliminate — see the animation notes below and §9.11.

**Visuals:** a rare card gets a persistent colored border/glow matching its tier — bold and clearly distinct tier-to-tier (thicker border + a background tint layered on the glow, not just a thin colored outline; Silver in particular uses a darker, more saturated blue-grey than the default card border so it can't be mistaken for "no rarity" at a glance), `card-view.js` / `.card--rarity-*` in `styles.css`. Bronze's glow color was tuned twice — the first pass (`#b5651d`) still read as "a duller gold" rather than a distinct tier to the owner, so it was changed to a more clearly muted, desaturated brown (`#8b5a2b`) that doesn't share gold's yellow warmth. A Joker-rarity card displays a jester face (🃏 + a flavor label) instead of its underlying rank/suit entirely — what it "counts as" is an internal wild-substitution detail the player doesn't need to see, they just see the resulting poker hand. **Bug found and fixed (owner report):** rarity styling used to apply to a card regardless of face-up/face-down state, so a face-down card's back would already hint at its rarity before it was ever flipped. `createCardElement()` now only attaches the rarity class when `faceUp` is true — confirmed via a live check that scans every face-down card for a leaked rarity class. **A second visibility bug found and fixed (owner report: "hard to see that it is selected other than when it is raised up")**: `.card--selected`'s border-color/box-shadow were being silently overridden by `.card--rarity-*`'s own border-color/box-shadow at equal CSS specificity (source order decided it, and the rarity rules came later in the stylesheet) — a selected rare card showed almost no selection indicator besides the lift. Fixed with compound `.card--rarity-*.card--selected` rules (higher specificity, order-independent) that layer a bright selection ring *inside* the rarity glow, so both read clearly at once.

**Reveal animation (owner request — "slower and more suspenseful and more dramatic," "feel special"):** a rare card's flip is slower than a common card's (180ms baseline → 350/500/700/1000ms for bronze/silver/gold/joker), preceded by a longer anticipation pause, a longer hold on the face-down back mid-flip (300/400/550/800ms — see §3k), and a glow-pulse animation (`.card--reveal-rare`, `@keyframes reveal-rare-pulse`) once it lands — verified live: the flip genuinely takes longer, the hold genuinely happens, and the pulse genuinely triggers, not just faster/slower text. Applies identically to a discard's replacement reveal and to the animated initial deal (§3k) — literally the same function, see below.

### 3i. Pity Points ✅ built (owner request, "funny")

A small, explicitly comedic consolation bonus (`pityBonus()` in `scoring.js`): once every other bonus is tallied *and the rarity multiplier applied* (§3l), if the running total is still under 250 points, add up to +150 more, scaling smoothly to 0 as the total approaches 250. A truly rock-bottom run (0 points from everything else) gets the full +150; a run that already cleared 250 gets nothing. Shown as its own "🎗️ Pity Points" line in the breakdown when it fires. Never large enough to matter strategically — it's a joke, not a rebalance.

### 3j. Score Reveal Order ✅ built, then re-laid-out (owner requests — "increase suspense", then "highest points at the top")

The score breakdown no longer reveals in category order (base → flavor → skill → extra → ...); `buildScoreLines()` in `board.js` sorts every line **ascending by point value**, so the reveal climbs from the smallest bonus up to the biggest number last — usually the base hand score, the new Hand-Rarity Synergy line (§3n), or a rarity bonus on a jackpot run. That part is unchanged.

**Layout follow-up (owner: "i want the highest points to be at the top and still appear last... 10 points appears, then above it comes 20 points and the 10 moves down").** Each new line used to simply append to the bottom of the list, so the reveal order and the final visual order were the same (smallest at top). Now `insertLineAtTop()` in `board.js` inserts each new line at the *top* instead, using the FLIP technique (record every existing line's position, insert, then transform it back to its old spot and transition that away to zero) so already-revealed lines visibly slide down to make room rather than jumping. Net effect: lines still reveal smallest-to-largest in time (suspense intact), but the *list* ends up biggest-at-top, smallest-at-bottom — a leaderboard-style layout. The static "already played" reload has no insertion sequence to produce that ordering, so it explicitly reverses `buildScoreLines()`'s array before rendering to land on the same final layout.

### 3k. Animated Initial Deal ✅ built, then unified with the discard reveal (owner request)

The opening hand no longer just appears. `initBoard()` in `board.js` renders all 5 cards face-down first (the "draw screen"), holds for a beat (~400ms), then flips each one face-up left to right. Cards aren't clickable and Lock In stays disabled until the whole sequence finishes — clicking mid-deal would force an instant re-render that'd spoil the cards still animating, so interaction is deliberately withheld until the reveal completes. Verified live frame-by-frame (Playwright): face-down at t=0, mid-flip during the cascade, fully interactive only once the last card lands.

**Unified with the discard/redraw reveal (owner follow-up: "i want the discard and draw animation to be the same as the initial draw animation").** These used to be two different animations that looked similar — the initial deal genuinely turned a face-down back into a face-up card, while a discard's replacement was a single continuous flip straight from the old face-up card to the new one, with no real "back" shown, just a rotated edge-on sliver at the midpoint. `flipReplaceCard()` in `animations.js` is now the *one* function both flows call, and it always does the same two-stage thing: turn to the actual face-down back (skipped if the card is already showing it, which is the initial-deal case), hold there for a beat (`HOLD_BY_RARITY` in `board.js`, 250ms common up to 800ms for a Joker), then turn to the face-up card. A discard's replacement now visibly shows its back and pauses before revealing, exactly like a freshly dealt card does — not an approximation of the same feeling, the literal same code path. Verified live: the discard-reveal sequence now passes through a genuine `.card--face-down` state mid-animation (confirmed via DOM polling), which it never did before.

### 3l. Score Scale Rebalance + Rarity Multiplier ✅ built (owner request, "make it feel fun to get a high number")

Owner reference point: RNGDLE, the game Cardle is modeled on, has typical outcomes around 2,000–8,000 points, with rare pulls reaching into the millions. Cardle's original scale (base hand 0–1000, everything else small and additive) topped out around 1,000–2,000 even with a Joker — a rare card felt like "a little bit higher," not a jump. Two changes, both needed together:

1. **Uniform ×5 rescale.** Every additive point constant in the game — the hand-rank table (§3a), Flavor Bonuses (§3b), Skill Bonuses' point values, Suit Synergy (§3e, including the per-rank number bonus), the extra-bonus registry (§3f), the rarity flat bonuses (§3h), and Pity Points (§3i) — was multiplied by the same factor. Uniform scaling was deliberate: it preserves every relative-balance decision already made (flavor staying minor relative to a made hand, suit synergy never outscoring a poker hand, pity staying a small safety net) exactly as before, just at a bigger scale, rather than re-deriving each relationship from scratch. A well-played hand (a made Flush or better, plus its usual bonuses) now lands squarely in the 2,000–8,000 target range without any rare card involved.

2. **New (this pass): a multiplicative Rarity Multiplier**, separate from the existing flat rarity bonus, applied to the whole additive total. Each rare card in the final hand contributed its own multiplier (§3h table) and they stacked multiplicatively.

Verified live at the time: a hand with two bronze cards and a Joker (multiplier 1.2 × 1.2 × 5 = 7.2×) landed at 35,928 points from an additive total of ~4,990.

**Superseded by §3m below, same session.** Multiplying the *whole* additive total meant a rare card paid off almost as well sitting in a worthless High Card (suit synergy/flavor/extra-bonuses fire regardless of hand strength) as in a genuinely made hand — which is exactly the follow-up complaint that produced §3m: *"i just got 900 points for 2 pair, but if i have no poker hands but one silver card, i get the same amount of points."* §3m keeps the *idea* (rare cards multiply, and stack multiplicatively) but changes *what* gets multiplied — only the base hand score — so the payoff now depends on actually having a hand to multiply.

Every score-scale-dependent constant elsewhere in the codebase was updated alongside this: achievement thresholds (§7b — High Roller 500→2,500, Zen Master's Full House+ bar, Full Send's Straight+ bar, Century Bonus's 100→500), personality thresholds (§7a — The Shark and The Architect's minimum final-hand score), and the Luck meter's calibration constants (§3g — `DEAL_QUALITY_CEILING`, `DRAW_FORTUNE_SCALE`, and the Risk meter's max-hand-score normalizer). Decision Rating (§3d) and the Skill meter needed **no changes** — both are ratios (score ÷ EV), so they're scale-invariant by construction; only things measured in *absolute* points needed touching.

### 3m. Odds-Calibrated Rarity + Hand-Rarity Synergy Bonus ✅ built (owner request — "do the math about the odds")

**The problem (owner report, immediately after §3l shipped):** *"i just got 900 points for 2 pair, but if i have no poker hands but one silver card, i get the same amount of points."* Diagnosis: §3l's multiplier applied to the *whole* additive total (base + flavor + suit synergy + skill + extra bonuses + rarity flat bonus), and suit synergy alone is pigeonhole-guaranteed on every 5-card hand — so a High Card with one rare card could reach a similar total to a genuinely made hand almost entirely on the back of bonuses that have nothing to do with either the hand or the rare card. The owner's ask had three parts: poker hands should be worth relatively more, combining a rare card *with* a made hand should be exponential, and — explicitly — "do the math about the odds of different poker hands and compare them to the odds of the rarities of the cards that are dealt."

**The math.** Standard 5-card poker probabilities (out of C(52,5) = 2,598,960) compared against the probability a hand contains *at least one* card of a given rarity tier (1-(1-chance)^5, independent per card):

| Outcome | Probability | Outcome | Probability |
|---|---|---|---|
| High Card | 50.12% | Two Pair | 4.75% |
| Pair | 42.26% | **Gold in hand** | **4.90%** |
| **Bronze in hand** | **30.43%** | Three of a Kind | 2.11% |
| **Silver in hand** | **14.13%** | **Joker in hand** | **1.00%** |
| — | — | Straight | 0.39% |
| — | — | Flush | 0.20% |
| — | — | Full House | 0.14% |
| — | — | Four of a Kind | 0.024% |
| — | — | Straight Flush | 0.0014% |
| — | — | Royal Flush | 0.00015% |

Sorted together, the striking result: **a gold card (4.90%) is actually slightly *more* common than Two Pair (4.75%)** — Two Pair is, in true rarity terms, harder to get than a gold card. And **every hand from Straight upward is rarer than even a Joker (1.00%)**. The old flat bonuses (§3l: gold up to 1,440, Joker flat 2,500) had this backwards — treating Joker as worth roughly a Straight Flush, when its real rarity sits between Three of a Kind and a Straight.

**Fix 1 — recalibrated flat bonuses.** Using "surprisal" (−log₂ probability, in bits) as a common scale for both hand ranks and rarity tiers, each tier's per-hand probability was located on the *existing* hand-score curve (interpolating between adjacent hand ranks by surprisal) and its `basePoints`/`perRankPoints` set so its average value (around rank 8) lands there: bronze ≈ 245 (between Pair and Two Pair, since 30% sits there), silver ≈ 351, gold ≈ 496 (right at Two Pair, matching the probability match above), Joker(common) ≈ 1,167 (between Three of a Kind and a Straight). The §3h table shows the resulting formulas. This is deliberately just the *modest*, unconditional "you kept a rare card" reward — it doesn't care whether the card contributed to anything.

**Fix 2 (this pass) — Hand-Rarity Synergy Bonus, replacing §3l's whole-total multiplier.** `scoreRun()` computed `handSynergyBonus = round(baseHandScore × (rarityMultiplier − 1))`, added as its own line, while everything else stayed purely additive. A High Card (base 0) with any rare card got exactly zero synergy; combining a rare card with a made hand scaled with the base hand score.

**Explicitly scoped out at the time, then requested anyway:** the original version of this section noted that "this specific card is structurally part of the scoring pattern" (a matched pair card vs. an unrelated kicker) wasn't being checked, reasoning that a rare card as a dead kicker in an otherwise-made hand "still benefits from that hand's strength, which feels fine." The owner's next message asked for exactly that distinction, with a precise worked example — see §3n, which supersedes this fix.

### 3n. Per-Card Attribution + Whole-Hand Multiplier ✅ built (owner request, with a precise worked example)

**The ask, verbatim (owner's own example):** *"if i had a 2 of hearts and a 2 of diamonds and then a random 3 of spades but it was a silver, it would not multiply the whole hand. but if i also had a 3 of diamonds that was normal and kept that silver 3 of spades, then it would multiply the entire hand including the original pair of 2's and the random 5th card."* In other words: Pair of 2s + an unrelated silver 3♠ kicker → no multiply. The same silver 3♠, but now paired with a normal 3♦ (making Two Pair, 2s-and-3s) → the silver 3♠ is genuinely *part of* the second pair, so the whole hand multiplies. This requires knowing not just *what hand you have*, but *which specific cards form it* — something §3m's baseScore-only synergy bonus didn't need, but this does.

**`contributingIndices()` (new, `hand-evaluator.js`)** — given the 5 final cards and the evaluated result, returns which card *indices* actually form the pattern: for Pair/Two Pair/Three of a Kind/Four of a Kind, just the matching-rank cards (via rank-count ≥ 2), excluding kickers; for Flush/Straight/Full House/Straight Flush/Royal Flush, all 5 (every card is load-bearing, no separate check needed). A wild Joker's contribution is evaluated against "whatever it wild-substituted to" (`evaluateHandWithWildJoker` now also returns `wildSubstitution: {rank, suit}`, exposing the winning substitution it was already computing internally) — a Joker in a Royal Flush is automatically "in the combo" because all 5 slots are, no special-casing needed; a Joker that only managed to complete a Pair is "in the combo" only if its substituted rank is one of the two matching ranks.

**`longestConsecutiveRunIndices()` (new, `bonus-registry.js`)** — the owner explicitly included Three Straight and Near Miss (§3f) alongside standard poker hands as qualifying "real combinations": *"any real poker hand plus a 3 straight and 4 straight."* This is the index-aware sibling of the existing `longestConsecutiveRun()` (which now just calls `.length` on this), tracing the longest run of consecutive ranks back to which original card positions form it — so a rare card that's part of a 3-straight/4-straight qualifies too, even when the overall hand is otherwise High Card.

**The gate — `hasRareCardInWinningCombo()` (`scoring.js`):** true if any rare card's index appears in `contributingIndices()` OR in a length-3/4 `longestConsecutiveRunIndices()` run. `scoreRun()` uses this as a binary switch: if true, the full stacked `rarityMultiplier()` (§3h) applies to the *entire* additive total (base + flavor + suit synergy + skill + extra bonuses + the rare card's own flat bonus + modifier) — replacing §3m's baseScore-only synergy bonus entirely. If false, there's no multiplier effect at all, just the flat `rarityBonus()`. `handSynergyBonus` (the line shown in the breakdown) is simply `multipliedTotal − additiveTotal` either way, so it's naturally 0 when the gate doesn't fire.

Verified live and in `tests/scoring.test.js` against the owner's own example: a Pair of 2s with an unrelated silver 3♠ kicker scores with **zero** synergy bonus; changing only the 5th card to a normal 3♦ (making the silver 3♠ genuinely part of Two Pair) multiplies the *entire* total by ×1.5. A Joker wild-completing a Royal Flush always qualifies (full-board hand); two independently-rolled Gold cards (one of them the Joker) in one Royal Flush stack to a ×6.25 whole-hand multiplier, comfortably clearing 30,000 points from a ~5,000-point hand — the "insane amount of points" the owner asked for, now gated on the rare card actually mattering rather than just being present.

### 3o. Multi-Rarity Scaling + Card Value Bonus ✅ built (owner request)

### 3s. Odds-Proportional Hand Score Rescale ✅ built (owner request — "a pair gives 200 but a royal flush gives 5000... only 25x more points")

**The problem.** §3l's ×5 rescale (5000/4000/3250/.../200/0) was a flat multiply of arbitrary v1 numbers — never actually derived from how rare each hand really is. The owner's complaint: Pair (42.26% of hands) and Royal Flush (0.00015%) are ~274,000x apart in real rarity, but only 25x apart in score. Presented three options (a moderate surprisal-linear curve capped ~15,000, an extreme odds-proportional curve reaching tens of millions, or a custom table); owner picked the extreme option.

**The math.** `HAND_RANKS` (`hand-evaluator.js`) is now odds-proportional: anchored at Pair = 200, every other score is `200 × (P(Pair) / P(that hand))` using the real 5-card poker probabilities out of C(52,5) = 2,598,960 (same probability table as §3m). Since score ∝ 1/probability, each hand's average payoff is now directly proportional to its true rarity:

| Hand | Score | Hand | Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Royal Flush | 54,800,000 | Straight | 21,600 |
| Straight Flush | 6,080,000 | Three of a Kind | 4,000 |
| Four of a Kind | 351,000 | Two Pair | 1,780 |
| Full House | 58,700 | Pair | 200 |
| Flush | 42,900 | High Card | 0 |

**Ripple effects — every other constant that had been hardcoded to a specific old `HAND_RANKS` value.** Auditing the codebase turned up that several "magic number" thresholds weren't actually arbitrary — they'd each been chosen to exactly equal some hand rank's old score (e.g. Perfect Keep's `900` was Three of a Kind, the Shark personality's `1750` was Flush), which would have silently gone stale (or meant something totally different) once the table above changed. Fixed by adding `scoreForHandId(id)` (`hand-evaluator.js`) and re-deriving every one of these from it instead of a bare number, so they can never drift out of sync again:
- `PERFECT_KEEP_THRESHOLD` (`scoring.js`) → Three of a Kind
- `highRoller`/`zenMaster` thresholds (`achievements.js`) → Full House; `fullSendClub` → Straight; `centuryBonus` → Two Pair
- Shark personality (`personality.js`) → Flush; Architect → Straight
- `MAX_HAND_SCORE` (`meters.js`, normalizes the Risk meter's chase bonus) → Royal Flush

**Luck meter recalibration.** `DEAL_QUALITY_CEILING`/`DRAW_FORTUNE_SCALE` (`meters.js`) were tuned against the old table's realistic bestEV range (~1750-2000) — meaningless once the underlying scale grew by orders of magnitude. Re-tuned empirically (`tools/calibrate-meters.js`, a one-off Monte Carlo script, same "empirical search" precedent as §3o's Pity Points fix): 1,000 sampled hands through the real `solveOptimalDiscard()` gave bestEV p50≈2,456/p90≈7,821/p95≈13,706 and |actualScore−chosenEV| p50≈2,256/p75≈4,671 under the new scale. Ceiling set to 8,000 (~p90, "a genuinely strong deal"), fortune scale to 5,000 (~p75), preserving the same relationship the old constants had to their own table.

**Number formatting.** Scores can now land in the tens of millions, so `animateCountUp()` (`animations.js`) and every other raw score display (score badges, the static "already played" header, share text) now render with `toLocaleString()` thousands-separators instead of a long unbroken digit string.

**Not touched:** rarity card bonus points (`rarity.js`'s `basePoints`/`perRankPoints`) — the owner's ask was specifically about the poker-hand table, not the rare-card flat bonuses, so those constants (bronze/silver/gold/diamond, ~150-1830) were left as-is rather than re-derived, keeping this change scoped to what was actually requested.

Verified live via Playwright against the dev server: custom-built a Pair (→ 200, matches table exactly), a Two Pair (→ 1,780), and a Royal Flush (→ 54,800,000, displayed as "54,800,000" with commas, total 54,801,425 once flavor/suit-synergy/skill bonuses stacked on top) — all three held pat with no discards. The Royal Flush correctly classified as "The Shark" personality (skill ≥ 90 and final-hand score ≥ the re-derived Flush threshold), confirming the re-anchored thresholds track the new scale correctly. Zero console errors on any of the three runs. 240/240 tests passing (existing hardcoded score assertions across `hand-evaluator`/`achievements`/`ev-solver`/`meters`/`personality`/`scoring` tests updated to reference `scoreForHandId()` instead of copied literals, so they can't silently drift out of sync with `HAND_RANKS` again either).

### 3v. Redeal-Mid-Animation Race Fixed ✅ fixed (owner bug report)

**The bug.** Owner: "if i am loading a deck and then click the new random hand button, it continues to flip the rest of the cards and also starts flipping the new cards." `dealInitialHand()` (`board.js`) is an async function with a `for` loop that awaits each card's flip in sequence, reading and writing `cardEls`/`originalHand` — both mutable variables in `initBoard()`'s outer closure, not local to that one call. Nothing stopped a second `startHand()` (fired by any admin-panel redeal/force/custom-hand button, all of which call it directly, with no guard against it already being mid-flight) from reassigning those same shared variables and clearing/rebuilding `handRow` while the FIRST call's loop was still `await`ing partway through. Both loops then kept running concurrently against the same 5 DOM slots — the old one flipping stale cards into elements the new deal had already replaced, the new one flipping its own cards on top — exactly the "flips the rest of the old cards AND starts flipping the new ones" the owner described. The identical structural flaw existed in `lockIn()` and `revealDrawnCards()` (the post-discard reveal), just harder to trigger — an admin redeal fired while a discard reveal or score calculation was still in flight would race the same way.

**The fix.** A single `dealToken` counter, bumped every time `startHand()` fires. `startHand()` captures its own value (`myToken`) before doing anything else and threads it into `dealInitialHand(myToken)`; `dealInitialHand` checks `token !== dealToken` after every `await` in its loop and returns immediately the moment it no longer matches — a newer deal has since started, so this one has nothing useful left to do. `lockIn()` and `revealDrawnCards()` got the same treatment (`lockIn` captures `dealToken` at entry as `token`, checked after the initial paint-yield and again after the reveal cascade; `revealDrawnCards` takes `token` as a parameter and checks it inside each card's flip callback). Nothing was cancelled mid-flight (a `flipReplaceCard()` promise already in progress still finishes its own animation) — the fix stops any *new* animation step or DOM write from a superseded run, so the stale loop just quietly stops advancing instead of fighting the current one for the same slots.

Verified live via Playwright, two scenarios: (1) clicking "Redeal with this luck" ~150ms into the opening deal, then immediately clicking "Show Gold" ~150ms after that (stress-testing back-to-back interrupts) — settled to exactly 5 cards, `Lock In` correctly re-enabled, no leftover elements from either interrupted deal; (2) locking in a discard, then redealing ~100ms into the post-discard reveal cascade — settled to exactly 5 cards with the result panel correctly still hidden (the interrupted run's score never got shown). Zero console errors in both. 249/249 tests passing (unchanged — this is DOM/timing-only, not covered by the core-logic unit tests, consistent with how `persistence.js`/`stats.js` are already verified via live smoke tests instead per §12).

### 3w. Three Straight / Four Straight Promoted to Real Hand Categories ✅ built (owner bug report: "i just got a three straight and the top said the hand was a high card")

**The problem.** Three Straight and Four Straight (formerly "Near Miss") existed only as extra bonuses (§3f, `bonus-registry.js`) — small stacking badges checked *independently* of the hand's own poker category. A hand whose best feature was a bare 3-run or 4-run, with no pair, still fell through `evaluateFixedHand()`'s category logic all the way to High Card, so the top of the page read "High Card" even while a "Three Straight" badge sat further down acknowledging the run. Reasonable complaint: if the game recognizes the run is special, the headline hand label should say so.

**The fix — promoted to real `HAND_RANKS` entries, positioned by actual rarity.** Computed exact probabilities by exhaustively enumerating all C(13,5)=1,287 distinct-rank, non-straight 5-card combinations and bucketing by longest consecutive run (Ace-low aware, same convention as the existing run-detection logic): a genuine 3-run (no pair, no straight) occurs in 13.7755% of hands, a 4-run in 3.1005% — both real, substantial slices of what used to be lumped into the 50.12% "High Card" bucket (the true High Card residue, run≤2, drops to 33.24%). Offered the owner a choice: rank these by strict odds-proportionality (matching every other category's calibration method, §3s) — under which Three Straight (13.78%) is actually *rarer than Pair* (42.26%) and so outranks it, and Four Straight (3.10%) outranks Two Pair but not Three of a Kind (2.11%) — or a simpler "always weaker than any made hand" placement. Owner picked strict odds-proportional, consistent with every prior rebalance this session.

New `HAND_RANKS` order (worst to best): High Card (0, deliberately flat — unaffected) → Pair (200) → **Three Straight (613)** → Two Pair (1,780) → **Four Straight (2,726)** → Three of a Kind (4,000) → Straight → Flush → Full House → Four of a Kind → Straight Flush → Royal Flush. Both new categories are rank-scaled by the run's own high card (§3u's convention, Ace-low aware — an A-2-3 run displays as high=3, not 14), with `TWO_PAIR`/`THREE_OF_A_KIND`'s rank-scaling constants recomputed since their neighbors changed (`PAIR`'s stayed identical — its binding neighbor, High Card, didn't move). Every adjacent-category boundary re-verified with zero overlap, same methodology as §3u.

**`evaluateFixedHand()`'s classification order is unchanged except for its final fallback**: any pair, straight, or flush still wins outright (a Three Straight/Four Straight can only be the *primary* hand when nothing else applies — the exact "High Card" branch this whole fix is about). `longestConsecutiveRunIndices()` (moved from `bonus-registry.js` into `hand-evaluator.js` — it's core classification logic now, not just a bonus heuristic; also gained a `highCard` field for rank-scaling) checks the run length there: 4 → Four Straight, 3 → Three Straight, otherwise High Card. `contributingIndices()` gained a case for both new ids, returning the run's own indices (not all 5 — there's still 1-2 dead kickers) so the score-breakdown proof strip and the rarity-multiplier's combo gate both highlight the right cards.

**Extra-bonus registry kept, but re-scoped to avoid double-crediting.** `bonus-registry.js`'s `threeStraight`/`nearMiss` (renamed **`fourStraight`**, per owner follow-up: "rename near miss to four straight also") now only fire when a run is hiding *inside* a stronger made hand (e.g. a Pair of 5s where 5-6-7-8 also happens to form a 4-run) — gated on `finalHandResult.id` not already being `THREE_STRAIGHT`/`FOUR_STRAIGHT`, since in that case it's already credited as the primary "HAND" badge. `achievements.js`'s Building Blocks achievement now fires on either path (the hand itself, or the coexisting bonus).

**Story generator gap caught by existing test infrastructure.** `FRAGMENTS.outcomeVerdict` (`src/story/templates.js`) is keyed by hand id and consumed unconditionally (`FRAGMENTS.outcomeVerdict[finalHandResult.id].map(...)`, no fallback) — the pre-existing `outcomeVerdict covers every poker hand category` test (which iterates `HAND_RANKS` dynamically) caught the gap immediately once the two new ids were added, exactly as designed. Added ~8 flavor lines each.

Verified live via Playwright with the owner's own scenario (8♠ 9♥ 10♦ + two unrelated cards): the page now reads **"Three Straight"** at the top instead of "High Card," with a HAND badge (+669, rank-scaled for the 8-9-10 run's high card of 10) and no separate/duplicate extra-bonus badge for the same run. 259/259 tests passing (7 new, covering classification, rank-scaling, the ordering invariant extended to both new categories, and the bonus-registry double-count guard).

### 3x. Flying Points Animation ✅ built (owner request: "cool animations on the actual cards showing the points come off the cards that gave the points and add into the total")

Each badge's card-proof strip (`highlightIndices`, DESIGN.md §3p) already knew which of the 5 hand-row cards a badge is attributed to — this connects that data to a visual effect instead of just a highlighted chip. Two pieces, both in the animated reveal path only (`revealScore()`, `board.js` — the static "already played" reload has no animation to hook into, unchanged):

**`flySparks()` (new, `animations.js`)** — for each badge, spawns one small glowing dot per contributing hand-row card (`document.getElementById('hand-row').children`, snapshotted once at the top of `revealScore()` since by then `revealDrawnCards()` has already finished replacing cards — stable for the whole reveal), each animating via a CSS keyframe (`score-spark-fly`, arcing slightly upward via a raised midpoint keyframe rather than a flat line) from the card's screen position to the score-total element's, fading and shrinking as it lands. Deliberately **no text on the spark itself** — a badge with 5 highlighted cards (a Flush's own HAND badge, Rainbow, Card Value, etc.) would otherwise show 5 identical "+N" chips flying in, misleadingly implying each card independently earned the full amount. The badge's own count-up and the running total already show the real numbers; the sparks are purely the visual thread connecting "these cards" to "this change," resolving once every spark lands so the total's count-up can start right on cue.

**`.card--score-pulse` (new, `styles.css`)** — a brief glow pulse (480ms, reusing the `--selected` accent color already used for card selection) applied to each contributing card the instant its badge starts revealing, giving the departure moment its own feedback beyond just the spark leaving. **Sequencing.** Per badge: the pulse fires and the sparks launch, running in parallel with the badge's own value count-up (`Promise.all`) rather than sequentially after it — both are roughly the same visual "moment," and running them concurrently keeps per-badge pacing close to what it was before (sparks ~550ms + stagger vs. the count-up's 350ms, so the added cost is the difference, not the sum). Zero console errors. No unit-test changes — this is DOM/timing-only, consistent with how the rest of the animation layer (§3v's `dealToken`, the flip/reveal system) is verified via live smoke tests rather than `npm test`.

### 3y. Three/Four Straight Classification Priority Fixed ✅ built (owner bug report: "a three straight should be alot more points like we discussed, it was only scoring 30 points for a j q k")

**The problem — §3w's fix was incomplete.** `evaluateFixedHand()`'s if-chain only ever reached the run-length check in its final fallback branch, *after* every pair-based check. But a Pair or Two Pair pattern can genuinely coexist with a longer consecutive run than the pattern itself implies — the paired rank can double as one end of the run (e.g. 5,5,6,7,8 is simultaneously "a Pair of 5s" and "a 5-6-7-8 Four Straight"; J,Q,K,4,4 is simultaneously "a Pair of 4s" and "a J-Q-K Three Straight"). Since the if-chain tested `rankCounts[0] === 2` before ever consulting run length, these hands always classified as the weaker Pair/Two Pair, with the run demoted to `bonus-registry.js`'s flat +30/+60 "extra bonus" badge instead of being the headline category it actually outranks — exactly the owner's report: a J-Q-K hand (with a coexisting pair among its kickers) displayed as "Pair" worth ~150 total, with only a throwaway "+30 Three Straight" badge acknowledging the run, not the ~750-point Three Straight category it should have been.

**The fix.** Reordered the classification if-chain to match `HAND_RANKS`' actual rarity ordering exactly: `FOUR_STRAIGHT` is now checked between Three of a Kind and Two Pair, and `THREE_STRAIGHT` between Two Pair and Pair — so whichever category is odds-proportionally *rarer* wins, regardless of chain position. Verified this can't create a NEW ambiguity: Three of a Kind (3 cards same rank + 2 kickers, max 3 distinct ranks) can never produce a 4-run; Two Pair (2 distinct pair ranks + 1 kicker, max 3 distinct ranks) can never produce a 4-run either — so inserting the Four Straight check before Two Pair never wrongly steals a genuine Two Pair hand. A Two Pair hand *can* coexist with a hidden 3-run (e.g. 7,7,8,8,9 — pairs of 7s/8s with 7-8-9 also a run) and correctly still wins as Two Pair (1,780 > Three Straight's 613), since that check still comes first.

**Performance regression caught and fixed before shipping.** Computing run length for every hand that reaches this deep into the chain (not just bare High Card candidates, as before) measurably slowed `ev-solver.js`'s exhaustive discard search — `tests/ev-solver.test.js`'s joker-search bound (52x wild-substitution search × thousands of discard/draw combinations) went from comfortably under 5000ms to ~5400-5600ms. Root cause: `longestConsecutiveRunIndices()` (Map allocations + two separate sorts, needed elsewhere for its card-index output) is overkill when `evaluateFixedHand()` only ever needs `{length, highCard}` for its own classification. Replaced with `runLengthAndHighCard()` (new), a flat `Uint8Array` presence-scan with no allocation beyond the array itself, memoized per call via a `getRun()` closure so it still only runs once even though two branches may need it. Net result: the full suite actually got *faster* than before this fix (~6000ms → ~3650ms), not just back to baseline.

**Test fallout, expected and fixed.** Several existing tests hard-coded the old (buggy) priority as intended behavior and needed updating to the corrected one: `hand-evaluator.test.js`'s "any pair still outranks a coexisting run" test was itself the bug, encoded as a passing assertion — replaced with tests asserting the correct winner in each direction (Four Straight over Pair, Three Straight over Pair, Two Pair still over a coexisting Three Straight). `bonus-registry.test.js`'s `fourStraight`/`threeStraight` extra-bonus tests used Pair-coexists-with-a-run examples that no longer stay classified as Pair — replaced with Flush/Three-of-a-Kind examples (categories that genuinely do outrank the run and can still coexist with one). `ev-solver.test.js`'s hand-verifiable EV scenario had an undocumented coincidence: two of its three "chase trips" kicker-discard branches happened to leave a pair-plus-hidden-3-run on the miss draw, so their EVs are no longer identical to the third (clean) branch — rewritten to derive each branch's expected EV from its own actual resulting hand rather than assuming symmetry that the classification fix broke.

Verified live via Playwright with both a bare J-Q-K run (J♦ Q♦ K♦ + 2♣ 7♥, no pair — already correctly "Three Straight" since §3w, since a bare run with zero pairs never hit the buggy branch) and a coexisting-pair case (5♠ 5♥ 6♦ 7♣ 8♠): the pair case now reads **"Four Straight"** with a 3,152 total instead of "Pair." The owner's exact dealt cards weren't captured in the bug report, but a J-Q-K hand only reaches the "+30 extra bonus, Pair at the top" state this fix targets if its two kicker cards happened to pair up with each other — the most likely explanation for the report, and the scenario this fix directly corrects either way. Zero console errors. 280/280 tests passing.

### 3x. Wild Becomes a Card Property, Not a Rarity Tier ✅ built (owner: "i want wilds to be normal cards that appear, not just rare cards, but i also still want it to have less of a chance of showing than a normal card by a little. it can still spawn with rarities tho")

Wild used to BE a rarity — `rarity: 'joker'` at 0.2% per card, sitting in the same ladder as Bronze and Gold, with a nested sub-roll deciding which tier it "counted as" for scoring. That made two unrelated ideas share one slot: a card could be wild, or rare, never both.

Wildness is now its own axis. A card rolls `wild` independently of `rarity`, so a Gold Wild is a real thing — it completes the hand *and* multiplies the score.

**Frequency is the owner's pick of "like a 53rd card in the deck":** 1.8% per card against an ordinary card's 1/52 = 1.92%. That is a Wild in roughly one hand in eleven, against one in a hundred before — a nine-fold increase, which is why the per-wild payoff was reduced to compensate. Every Wild pays a flat bonus (the owner's choice over none), sized at roughly what a Bronze pays. It deliberately carries **no multiplier of its own**: at this frequency, stacking a whole-score multiplier on top of "it completes a hand it had no business completing" would make wilds the dominant scoring force rather than a lucky break. A wild that also rolls a tier still gets that tier's multiplier, because that is the rarity paying out, not the wildness.

**The RNG call count per card is unchanged at two.** The second used to pick a joker's sub-tier and now decides wildness, so a given seed still deals exactly the same ranks and suits it always did — only what those cards rolled has changed. That kept the blast radius to rarity/wildness assertions instead of every seeded hand in the suite.

**Backwards compatibility is not optional here.** Every hand already stored — in localStorage and in `daily_plays` — records its wild as `rarity: 'joker'` with a `jokerTier`, and those rows are still read: the profile renders them, and the leaderboard RE-EVALUATES them to name the hand (§11n). So `isWild()` is the single definition and accepts both shapes; `pointsForRarity`/`multiplierForRarity` still resolve the retired id; and the card renderers still draw a legacy wild with its tier ring. Without that, every historical wild would silently stop being wild and quietly downgrade old hands on the boards.

**Reveal drama needed rethinking too.** Pacing was keyed off the rarity id, and wild was one. A plain Wild is still a moment, so `revealDramaFor()` takes the SLOWER of the card's rarity pacing and the wild pacing — the rarer thing sets the beat — and the glow now fires for a plain wild as well as a rare card.

**The How to Play modal was rewritten around the same split** (owner: "make the how to play instructions better. say something about what the goal is and separate the wild from the rare card"). It now opens with the goal stated plainly, then the steps, then Wild and rare cards as two separate sections. Wild gets a worked example — `9♠ 9♥ 4♦ 🃏` plays as a third nine; four hearts and a Wild finishes the flush — because "it becomes what you need" is abstract until you see it. The old copy listed Wild as the fourth item in "🥉 Bronze, 🥈 Silver, 🥇 Gold, 🃏 Wild", which is exactly the framing that made it read as just another rare tier.

**Verified** with 34 browser assertions and the existing unit suites: a wild completing Three of a Kind where the same hand without it is a Pair, a legacy stored wild still evaluating as wild, a plain wild paying exactly its flat bonus as one row, a Gold Wild paying both as two rows, all five render permutations (ordinary / rare / plain wild / rare wild / legacy wild), and the help text's claims matching what scoring actually does.

**One copy bug caught by writing the test.** The first draft said a Wild "isn't worth points for being wild" — true of the design I had assumed, false of the one the owner picked. There is now an assertion that the modal does not deny the bonus, so the copy cannot drift back out of step with the scorer.

**And one of my own.** The new plain "Show Wild" admin button carried only `data-force-wild`, while the click handler was bound to `[data-force]` — so it rendered perfectly and did nothing. Found by driving the real button rather than trusting that it existed.

### 4a. First Pass — 5 Modifiers ✅ built (owner: "i think we should next work on the daily modifiers")

**Scope, decided up front.** 17 named modifiers, most with only a one-line v1 description, no registry, no daily-selection logic, no UI — too much for one pass. Picked 5 that each exercise a genuinely different hook point without inverting the hand hierarchy achievements/personality/meters all assume "higher = better" against (Lowball/Reverse Rankings specifically do invert it, and are deliberately deferred to their own pass once this pattern is proven): **Suit Bonus** (additive scoring), **Flush Frenzy** (multiplicative-feeling scoring, expressed additively), **One Swap** (discard limit ↓), **Fourth Chance** (discard limit ↑), **Locked Card** (a specific card can't be discarded).

**Every day has exactly one active modifier** (GAME_PLAN.md: "Every day should include one modifier that changes strategy") — no "vanilla" day. `src/core/modifiers.js`, new: `MODIFIERS` registry + `getDailyModifier(date)`, the single entry point.

**Rotation — a real bug caught and fixed before it shipped.** First implementation independently reshuffled fixed non-overlapping 5-day blocks (one shuffle per block). Looked right in isolation but broke the *actual* no-repeat requirement: two independently-shuffled blocks can freely place the same modifier 1-2 days apart straddling the seam between them — caught by testing 10 consecutive days directly (`fourthChance` showed up on both day 1 and day 3, only 2 days apart). Replaced with a greedy sliding-window construction: day D's modifier is a seeded pick among whichever modifiers did *not* appear in the immediately preceding (roster size − 1) days, which is the strongest guarantee possible with exactly 5 modifiers — every window of 5 consecutive days is a full permutation. Side effect, not a bug: with exactly `roster size` modifiers, the exclusion window always leaves exactly one legal candidate once it fills, so the sequence settles into a fixed repeating cycle (period 5) after a short seeded ramp-up — there's no other way to guarantee a repeat-free 5-day window with only 5 possible values. This stops being deterministic once the roster grows past its own window size; real day-to-day choice returns once there are more modifiers than the no-repeat target. Pure function of the calendar date throughout (an in-memory loop from a shared project epoch forward, same one `persistence.js`'s `dayNumber()` uses) — no persistence, matching every other daily mechanic in the app. Verified: zero repeats in any 5-day trailing window across 40 sampled consecutive days; stays fast (<200ms) even for a date over a decade out.

**Suit Bonus and Locked Card each need their own extra daily-random pick** (which suit; which of the 5 card slots) — drawn from a seed namespace completely separate from the deck's own dealing RNG (`cardle-modifier-<day>-<purpose>`, vs. the hand deal's `cardle-<day>`), so adding/changing modifiers can never perturb the existing daily-hand-determinism guarantee or any test built around its exact RNG call sequence.

**Suit Bonus and Flush Frenzy both plug into scoring.js's existing flat `modifierBonus` param** — no new scoring.js plumbing needed for either. Flush Frenzy's "flushes score double" is exactly "add another copy of the Flush's own base score" when the final hand IS a Flush, so it's a flat additive amount like Suit Bonus, not a real multiplier. `scoreRun()`'s `modifierBonus` param was extended (backward compatibly) to also accept a `(finalHandResult, finalHand) => number` callback, not just a plain number — `modifiers.js`'s `modifierScoringBonus(dailyModifier)` returns exactly that shape, letting the bonus be computed from the `finalHandResult` `scoreRun()` already evaluates internally, rather than the caller needing to run `evaluateHand()` a second time itself (which would double the cost of a wild-Joker hand's already-expensive 52-substitution search, §3h).

**Locked Card needed one new solver capability.** `ev-solver.js`'s `solveOptimalDiscard()` gained an optional `excludedIndices` param — without it, "best possible EV" would silently include discard combinations touching the locked card, a play the player was never actually allowed to make, corrupting the Decision Rating baseline for that entire day.

**UI**: a pill-shaped banner (`#modifier-banner`, new) under the day label, always visible, showing today's emoji/label/description — including on the "already played" reload path, since the banner render happens before that early return. `createCardElement()` (`card-view.js`) gained a `locked` option: a dashed border, a 🔒 badge (opposite corner from the rarity badge, so both can show at once), `disabled = true`, and no click handler attached at all — belt-and-suspenders with `toggleDiscard()`'s own early-return guard. The discard-count hint text and `MAX_DISCARDS` (renamed `DEFAULT_MAX_DISCARDS`, now just the fallback) everywhere in `board.js` now read from the day's resolved `maxDiscards` instead of a hardcoded 3.

**The real calendar date by default, admin-overridable** — same reasoning as the caption pool's daily rotation (§6d): a normal redeal (luck slider, force-rarity buttons, custom hand builder) never changes which day it is, so the active modifier stays fixed across those. §4b below adds an explicit admin override on top of this default.

Verified live via Playwright (using a faked `Date` to preview modifiers other than the real day's, before §4b's in-panel picker existed): **One Swap** (the real current day) correctly caps at 1 discard, confirmed via the hint text; **Locked Card** shows exactly one `.card--locked` card, and clicking it (even forcing past Playwright's normal actionability check) never selects it; **Flush Frenzy** on a dealt Flush shows a "Modifier Bonus: +48,165" badge exactly matching the Flush's own "+48,165" base badge, confirming the double. Zero console errors across all three. 277/277 tests passing (18 new: `tests/modifiers.test.js` covering determinism, the no-repeat guarantee, both scoring modifiers' payouts, and `tests/ev-solver.test.js`'s new `excludedIndices` coverage).

### 4c. Modifiers Rewritten to Multiply the Total, Not Add Flat Points ✅ built (owner: "i think the modifiers should add multipliers to the total if the modifier that is used is in the winning hand, not just bonus points that dont really contribute to the total score")

**The problem.** §4a's Suit Bonus (+60/matching card) and Flush Frenzy (+the Flush's own base score, "reads as doubling") were both flat additive amounts — reasonable at the old point scale, but the odds-proportional/rank-scaled hand-rank table (§3s/§3u) now puts most winning totals in the thousands-to-millions range, so a flat +60 (or even a few hundred) is essentially invisible against the total. The owner explicitly wanted these to scale WITH the hand, not sit as a small fixed add-on.

**The fix — `scoreRun()`'s `modifierBonus` param became `modifierMultiplier`.** Same callback shape as before (`(finalHandResult, finalHand) => number`, or a plain number), but now multiplies rather than adds, applied on top of the existing rarity-synergy multiplier (§3n) and before Pity Points, so it's part of `total` — not a separate line item bolted on afterward. Defaults to `1` (no-op) so every caller can apply it unconditionally.

- **Suit Bonus**: was `+60` flat per matching card; now `+50%` (`×0.5`) per matching card in the final hand — a full 5-card flush of the bonus suit reaches `×3.5`. Zero matching cards naturally multiplies by `×1` (no effect), so the modifier only ever swings the total when its condition is genuinely in the winning hand, matching the owner's framing exactly. (Launched at `+20%`/`×2.0` max; bumped to `+50%`/`×3.5` max after an immediate owner follow-up: "i think it should be a higher multiplier than it currently is.")
- **Flush Frenzy**: was "+the Flush's own base score" (arithmetically equal to doubling, but expressed additively and only touching the base score); now a real `×4` applied to the *entire* total — base hand score, flavor, suit synergy, card value, skill bonuses, extra bonuses, rarity bonuses, all of it — when the final hand IS a Flush. (Launched at `×2`; also bumped in the same follow-up.)
- `modifiers.js`'s `modifierScoringBonus()` renamed **`modifierScoringMultiplier()`** to match; `discardLimit`/`lockedCard`-type modifiers still return `1` (no scoring effect — their whole effect lives in board.js's discard rules).

**UI**: the score-breakdown's flat "Modifier Bonus" badge became **"×N Modifier Multiplier"** (mirroring the existing "×N Hand-Rarity Synergy" badge's own label style, §3n), showing the actual point delta the multiplier contributed (`total_after − total_before`), not a fixed amount.

**Suit icon fix, same request.** Suit Bonus's description previously interpolated the raw suit code (`ctx.bonusSuit`, e.g. `"D"`) directly into the sentence — "Every D card..." — instead of the card's actual suit symbol. Now uses `deck.js`'s existing (already-exported) `suitGlyph()` helper: "Every ♦ card in your final hand adds +50% to your final score today."

Verified live via Playwright at both magnitudes: at launch, a Suit Bonus preview ("Every ♠ card...") against a custom 3-spade hand produced a "×1.6 Modifier Multiplier: +219" badge (base additive total 365 × 1.6 = 584, exactly matching the displayed grand total), and Flush Frenzy against a dealt Flush produced "×2 Modifier Multiplier: +49,230" (49,230 × 2 = 98,460). After the bump, the same Flush Frenzy hand's base additive total (49,230, unchanged) now produces "×4 Modifier Multiplier: +147,690" for a 196,920 grand total, exactly matching the new `×4`. Zero console errors either time. 280/280 tests passing throughout (`modifiers.test.js` and `scoring.test.js` rewritten for the multiplier semantics, then re-verified against the bumped constants).

### 4d. Second Pass — 3 More Modifiers ✅ built (owner: "i think we should add a couple more modifiers. i think one should be you have 2 rounds of discards but the second should be less than the first")

**Clean Slate** (🧽, `type: 'discardLimit'`, `maxDiscards: 5`) — the simplest of the three, a pure reuse of the exact hook One Swap/Fourth Chance already use. Discard up to all 5 cards; Perfect Keep (§3, "held all 5 and still landed an excellent hand") is naturally unreachable that day since holding pat isn't really an option worth taking, same self-balancing behavior noted for this modifier back when it was still just a roster placeholder.

**High Roller** (🎩, `type: 'scoring'`) — a flat `×1.5` on the total, every run, regardless of hand. The first "always-on" modifier in the roster: Suit Bonus/Flush Frenzy's `modifierScoringMultiplier()` branches are gated on a hand condition (matching suit cards present / final hand IS a Flush) and fall back to `×1`; High Roller's branch just returns `1.5` unconditionally. Deliberately simpler than the original one-line roster placeholder ("×1.5, bonuses disabled") — "bonuses disabled" would mean re-plumbing `scoreRun()` to conditionally skip flavor/suit synergy/skill bonuses/rarity on High Roller days, a much bigger change for a twist that reads the same to the player either way; owner picked this simplified version explicitly when scoping the pass.

**Second Look** (👀, `type: 'twoRoundDiscard'`, `round1MaxDiscards: 3`, `round2MaxDiscards: 1`) — the one genuinely new mechanic, not a reuse of an existing hook. The whole rest of the game only ever supported ONE discard/draw/lock-in pass; this required an actual second round of player interaction, not just a different config value.

- **The flow.** Round 1 behaves exactly like a normal day, capped at 3 discards, except the button reads "Draw (Round 1 of 2)" instead of "Lock In" and clicking it (new `startRoundTwo()`) only replaces the marked cards and reveals them (reusing the exact same `revealDrawnCards()` flip animation a normal lock-in already uses) — no EV solve, no scoring, no persistence. That reveal becomes the new `originalHand`; `drawPile` drops the cards just consumed; `maxDiscards` drops to the modifier's `round2MaxDiscards` (1); a fresh `discardRound` counter flips to 2. From there, round 2 is *exactly* the existing single-round flow, completely unchanged code — same `lockIn()`, same `solveOptimalDiscard()`, same `scoreRun()`.
- **Only round 2 is judged.** Since round 2's `lockIn()` treats the post-round-1 hand as its own `originalHand`, every downstream system (Decision Rating, Luck/Skill/Risk meters, personality, achievements, Perfect Keep/Long Shot/Clean Finish) grades round 2's decision only — round 1 reads as an ungraded "mulligan" that reshapes the starting hand rather than a play that itself gets scored. Deliberate simplification over building a true joint two-round EV solver (which would mean solving an expectimax over "for every round-1 discard × every possible draw, what's the best round-2 EV from there" — a combinatorially much bigger search than the existing single-pass exhaustive solver, and a real risk to the existing perf budget, §3y). The persisted `originalHand` (and anything derived from it, like the story caption) reflects the post-round-1 hand too, for the same reason.
- **New state, `board.js`**: `discardRound` (1 or 2, reset every fresh deal alongside `selected`), `isTwoRoundModifier()`, `updateLockInButtonLabel()`, `startRoundTwo()`. `applyModifier()` now special-cases `type === 'twoRoundDiscard'` for its *initial* `maxDiscards` (round 1's cap, since there's no flat `.maxDiscards` field on this modifier shape). `updateHint()` appends "(Round 1 of 2)"/"(Round 2 of 2 — final)" so the discard cap displayed always matches which round is active.
- **No new scoring.js/ev-solver.js plumbing at all** — the entire feature is a `board.js`-only state-machine addition on top of already-existing pieces (`revealDrawnCards`, `lockIn`, `renderHand`, `toggleDiscard`), which is what kept it tractable in one pass despite being a bigger lift than Clean Slate/High Roller.

### 4e. Double or Nothing ✅ built (owner: "make one where it reveals 2 cards, and then you can do a double or nothing based on those cards seen, and then it reveals the last 3 cards, im not sure if they should be allowed to discard cards on this one or not, you decide what would be more fun")

**The discard-or-not call, made and explained rather than asked.** Owner explicitly delegated this one: allowing discards on both paths would make the wager feel like a minor flavor addition on an otherwise-normal round; allowing none anywhere would remove the game's core mechanic entirely for the day. Landed on an asymmetric split instead — **going for it locks in exactly the 5 dealt cards, no discards at all** (real tension: you bet on a hand you can no longer change), **playing it safe is a completely ordinary round** (normal discard cap, no multiplier, no risk). A genuine fork in what kind of day it is, rather than a watered-down version of the same round either way.

**The flow, `type: 'peekWager'`.** `dealInitialHand()` now branches for this modifier: flips only cards 0-1 face-up (the shared flip loop was extracted into `flipCardsInRange(cards, startIndex, endIndex, token)` for this, reused by the normal full-hand deal too — same animation either way, just a different range), then shows a new prompt (`#wager-prompt`, new markup in `index.html`) with two buttons instead of enabling the hand for interaction. Picking a side calls `resolveWager(wagered)`: stamps `dailyModifier.wagered = wagered` directly onto the resolved modifier object (same "extra field on the object at decision time" pattern Locked Card's `.lockedIndex` already uses), reveals cards 2-4 via the same shared flip helper, then either locks in immediately with `maxDiscards = 0` (going for it — reusing `lockIn()` completely unchanged, with an empty discard set) or resets to the normal discard cap and hands off to the exact same interactive flow every other day uses (playing it safe).

**Scoring — Pair or better to keep the double, a bare High Card and it's zero.** `modifiers.js`'s `modifierScoringMultiplier()` reads `dailyModifier.wagered`: not wagered (or not yet decided) → `1` (no-op, the same default every other modifier falls back to); wagered and `handStrengthIndex(finalHandResult.id) >= handStrengthIndex('PAIR')` → `2`; wagered and still just a High Card → `0`. The threshold isn't an arbitrary new number — it's exactly the boundary HIGH_CARD's own "deliberately worthless" `score: 0` (hand-evaluator.js §3s) already treats as a miss, so "or nothing" lines up with a cutoff the game already had rather than inventing a second one.

**A real bug this modifier's ×0 case exposed, caught before shipping.** `scoreRun()` already handled a 0 multiplier correctly (`total` came out right — verified directly against `scoring.test.js`), but the *displayed* total in the browser was wrong: 925 shown instead of the correct 270 for a losing High Card hand. Root cause: `revealScore()`'s running total isn't a re-read of `score.total` — it's independently accumulated by summing exactly the badges `buildScoreBadges()` returns (`running += badge.value` per badge, animated one at a time). Every modifier before this one only ever multiplied *up*, so the Modifier Multiplier badge's push condition was `score.modifierBonusAmount > 0` — harmless when the delta can only be positive, but a ×0 wipe produces a *negative* delta (`0 − additiveTotal`), which that condition silently excluded from the badge list, so the running total never subtracted it and drifted to "as if the multiplier were 1" instead of "0." Fixed by changing the condition to `!== 0` and rendering the negative case as a distinct **"💥 Busted — Nothing"** badge (own emoji/label/description) instead of reusing the "×N Modifier Multiplier" framing, which wouldn't make sense for a ×0. Both `badgeCardHtml()` (the static "already played" reload path) and the animated reveal loop's per-badge count-up had a hardcoded `+` prefix that also needed to go sign-aware (`toLocaleString()` already renders negative numbers correctly on its own — the bug was the forced `+` glued in front of it, e.g. "+-655"). Locked in with a new `scoring.test.js` case asserting `modifierBonusAmount` goes negative and matches the shortfall exactly; the UI fix itself has no unit coverage (board.js DOM isn't unit-tested in this project) and was verified live instead.

Verified live via Playwright across all three paths, using the admin panel's modifier picker plus Custom Hand Builder together (redeal with Double or Nothing active, then use the custom builder to set an exact hand — the modifier stays sticky across that second redeal, same as every other admin tool): **going for it, winning** — a custom Pair hand showed "×2 Modifier Multiplier: +808" for a 1,616 total, matching base×2 exactly; **going for it, busting** — a custom bare-High-Card hand (2♠ 5♥ 9♦ J♣ K♠, confirmed via reading the actual rendered card corners, not just the label) showed a new "💥 Busted — Nothing: -655" badge and a correct 270 total (pity-floor only) — this is the exact case that caught the badge bug above, first showing an incorrect 925 before the fix, then a correct 270 after; **playing it safe** — the same High Card hand under Play It Safe showed a completely normal round (discard, redraw, no modifier badge at all). Zero console errors across every case. 290/290 tests passing.

### 4f. Third Pass — Four Scoring Modifiers, a Leaderboard One, and One Removed ✅ built (owner: "if this card is held at the end then multiply by a certain amount... rainbow modifier (if you have one of each of all suits)... even/odd cards give more... flipped leaderboard day, normal draw but lowest score is on top for that day... modifier for each hand type (ex. pair gives more that day)... remove one swap modifier")

Six changes. Five of them reuse existing hooks; one is a genuinely new kind of modifier.

**Held Card is the inverse of Locked Card, deliberately.** Both mark a slot, but Locked Card *forbids* discarding it while Held Card leaves it entirely up to you — the ⭐ is a bribe (×2 on the whole score if it survives), not a rule. That makes it the first modifier whose interesting decision is purely voluntary: the marked card is often the one you most want to throw away. The badge sits on the opposite corner from 🔒, uses a solid gold ring against the lock's dashed grey one, and pointedly does *not* dim the card or disable the button, since nothing should suggest the slot is unavailable.

It needed the one structural change in this batch. "Did you keep it?" is not answerable from the final hand — a replacement occupies the same index — so `modifierScoringMultiplier`'s callback gained an optional third argument carrying `discardIndices`. A context bag rather than more positional parameters, and defaulted, so every existing caller and test is unaffected.

**Rainbow (all four suits, ×2.5) is priced above Held Card** because it actively fights every flush-shaped hand; **Even Money** pays per matching card like Suit Bonus but at half the rate (0.25 vs 0.5), since roughly half the deck matches a parity where only a quarter matches a suit. Both read the LOGICAL hand, so a Wild counts as the suit it actually plays as rather than its meaningless dealt one — the §3t bug, avoided by following the existing rule rather than rediscovering it.

**Hot Hand is one modifier that names a different category each day**, not twelve separate ones — the same shape Suit Bonus already uses for its suit, chosen so hand-type days stay a rotation event rather than dominating the roster. It picks from `HAND_RANKS` itself, so a future category is automatically eligible. Two are excluded: `HIGH_CARD`, which scores 0 by design (multiplying zero is still zero, so the day would read as broken), and `ROYAL_FLUSH`, which is rare enough that the bonus would effectively never pay. Matching is EXACT — "Two Pair scores ×4 today" means Two Pair. Flush Frenzy spans every flush because Straight and Royal Flush genuinely *are* flushes; no equivalent family relationship exists here.

**Upside Down is the first modifier that changes nothing about playing a hand.** The daily board ranks lowest-first; the score, its grade, and the career points it contributes are exactly what they would be on any other day. That containment is the whole design: DESIGN.md §12 deferred Lowball precisely because achievements, personality, the decision rating and the EV solver all assume higher is better, and none of them are touched here. What it creates instead is a real tension — topping today's board and maximising career points pull in opposite directions.

It could not be done in the client. The SQL returns the top N by score DESC, so reversing those rows client-side yields the *bottom of the top 25*, not the 25 genuinely lowest — the rows needed were never sent. Migration 011 adds a `sort_ascending` parameter, applied before the limit. The per-player `distinct on` follows the same direction, so an ascending board represents a player by the row that would actually rank them; academic on a one-play-per-day board, but leaving it inconsistent would break silently the moment the flag met a wider window. The flag is clamped to the daily board in `fetchLeaderboard` rather than trusted from the caller, since one day's rule must never reorder scores from days it has nothing to do with. The board carries a note explaining itself — a leaderboard whose rank 1 is the worst score is otherwise indistinguishable from a sorting bug — and the footer says "Bottom 25".

**One Swap was removed.** Nothing migrates: a stored admin override naming it is dropped by `validateModifierOverrides` as an unknown id, which is exactly the degrade-to-the-rotation behaviour that validation exists for.

**A bug found while testing, not reported.** The server config read is async, so on a slow connection it resolved *after* a tester had already picked a modifier to preview — and overwrote it a second later, which reads as the preview button not working. An explicit action now outranks a fetch that was already in flight. Found because the test suite was accidentally hitting the real Supabase project, where a pinned modifier kept replacing the previewed one; the suite is now hermetic, which it should have been from the start.

## 6. Caption Creator (formerly "Poker Story Generator") — highest investment area ✅ reworked

This is the feature most likely to become Cardle's identity, the way RNGDLE's poems are its identity. Treat it as first-class, not a launch-day nice-to-have.

### 6a. First rework attempt — freeform 3-line pools (superseded by §6c, kept for history)

Shipped, then superseded in the very same session once the owner saw it in action and asked for a different shape (§6c) — kept here for the reasoning trail, since the *why* (RNGDLE's captions work because the words have personality, not because they narrate the hand) carried forward even though the *format* didn't.

**The critique.** The original design (§6b below, kept for history) had each of 6 slots semantically pinned to a fact about the hand — which discard count, how much the draw improved things, which exact poker category landed. Owner shared an external write-up arguing this was the wrong model entirely: RNGDLE's captions aren't good because they narrate the hand accurately, they're good because the *words* have personality (poetic, dumb, internet-humor, absurd, all mixed together) and *don't have to make sense together* — "folded three kings, cried anyway" isn't a poker recap, it's a vibe. Explicit owner ask: "I would not limit players to making a poker story. That's too restrictive... make each dropdown just a collection of phrases that can be mixed into anything."

**Scope, decided up front.** The source material bundled two different projects — the caption rework itself, and a whole achievement-gated/rarity/seasonal phrase-unlock progression system (essentially a "Collection Book," §7's still-not-built item). Owner agreed to split them: this pass is the caption rework only; the unlock/collection system is deferred as its own future project rather than bundled in.

**New structure — 6 independent flat pools, not narrative beats.** `mood`, `action`, `object`, `descriptor`, `outcome`, `tag` (`src/story/templates.js` `FRAGMENTS`), assembled the same visual way as before — 3 lines, two slots each, `${a}, ${b}` — but the CONTENT is now unrelated phrase fragments, not sentence halves that need to agree grammatically: `mood, action` / `object, descriptor` / `outcome, tag`. Example real output: *"financially irresponsible, respectfully declined reality / the flop merchant, an all-gas-no-brakes raccoon / an honest L, big if true."* None of it has to parse as a sentence — that's the point, not a bug.

**Only `outcome` reacts to the actual hand (owner: "loosely tied," not fully decoupled).** `mood`/`action`/`object`/`descriptor`/`tag` are the exact same pool regardless of what happened this run. `outcome` alone splits into a `good`/`bad` pool based on `handStrengthIndex(finalHandResult.id) >= handStrengthIndex('TWO_PAIR')` — Two Pair or better reads as a real win ("somehow won," "certified banger," "we ball"), anything weaker reads as a loss ("skill issue," "an honest L," "internally screaming"). This was the explicit alternative to full decoupling, picked over it so there's still some payoff tied to the hand you actually played.

**Vocabulary blends the tones the critique called out**: poetic/absurd (*"a probability goblin," "spiritually rich"*), internet humor (*"no thoughts head empty," "huge if true," "we ball"*), and poker-specific memes (*"folded pocket aces," "river merchant," "dealer diff," "went full nit"*). ~14–18 options per pool (up from the old design's hard ~10 cap — owner's rework explicitly wants more variety; `tests/story-generator.test.js` now caps at 25/pool instead of 10, guarding against unbounded growth rather than a tight ceiling).

**Content bug caught by a new test, live-verified before shipping:** three phrases (*"dealer diff, the good kind," "respectfully, an L," "no thoughts, head empty"*) had an internal comma — harmless alone, but every phrase already gets joined to a neighbor with a comma at the line level, so these produced a confusing triple-comma line (*"an honest L, respectfully, big if true"*-style). Fixed the three phrases and added a permanent regression test (`no fragment has an internal comma`) so this class of authoring mistake can't silently ship again.

**Dropped as part of the decoupling, deliberately not carried forward:** `describeNotableGroup()`'s `{word}` template-filling (e.g. "two kings" spliced into a structural fragment) — there's no more "structural" fragment category for it to fill, and reintroducing hand-specific substitution into an otherwise-decoupled pool would partially undo the rework's own premise. `src/story/vocabulary.js` (`RANK_VOCAB`/`SUIT_VOCAB`) was left in place untouched rather than deleted — it's separately documented (§6b below, §5) as forward-looking infrastructure for the not-yet-built Theme vocabulary injection, unrelated to this rework, not leftover cruft from it.

**Everything else about the feature is unchanged**: the player-built dropdown picker (`renderStoryBlock`, `board.js` — `STORY_SLOT_ORDER` updated to the 6 new slot names, nothing else about the UI needed to change), the hash-seeded deterministic starting selection with no cross-day memory, the emoji grid and "📋 Copy Result" share block, one line per pair with `white-space: pre-line`.

Verified live via Playwright: dealt both a weak hand (High Card → confirmed `outcome.bad` pool) and a strong one (Two Pair → confirmed `outcome.good` pool), confirmed all 6 dropdowns render with their new labels ("Mood," "The move," "The target," "The vibe," "The verdict," "The tag") and correct option counts, confirmed picking a different Mood option updates the caption text live, confirmed Copy Result still works. Zero console errors. 255/255 tests passing.

### 6b. Original design (superseded by §6a/§6c, kept for history)

- Three-beat structure: **Setup → Turning Point → Outcome**, matching the tone of the brief's and owner's examples ("The kings refused to fold. An eight wandered into the family, and fortune rewarded stubbornness.").
- **Direct player-built phrase picker (owner request — "different options of little phrases that I can add together to make my own story," explicitly *not* an auto-picked tone):** each beat is assembled from two independently-chosen pieces — a **structural** fragment tied to the hand's facts (discard count, how much the hand improved, final poker category) plus a **flavor** fragment that's free mood/humor, joined as `${structural}, ${flavor}`. Rather than an auto-picked "voice" choosing the flavor fragments, **the player is shown all 6 pieces as dropdowns and picks each one directly** — the assembled story updates live as they change any piece.
- `describeNotableGroup()` fills the one `{word}` slot structural fragments need — the hand's biggest matching-rank group ("two kings"), or its high card if there's no pair.
- Rank/suit flavor vocabulary in `src/story/vocabulary.js`: Aces → "legends", Kings → "royalty", 2s → "the deuce"; Spades → "shadow", Hearts → "passion", Diamonds → "fortune", Clubs → "iron" — filled out for every rank 2–14. Left in place as future Theme-vocabulary infrastructure even though the caption generator itself no longer calls it.
- **No cross-day memory (owner reversal):** an earlier pass remembered the player's last pick per category across days (`src/state/preferences.js`). Owner explicitly asked to drop that — "don't save the last picked phrases, let them each day be selected and randomized." `preferences.js` was removed; the hash-seeded default is the *only* source of the starting selection, every day, with nothing written to `localStorage` for it.
- Emoji summary grid: **removed from the visible UI** (owner request — it sat above the story and wasn't wanted there). The underlying generator (`buildEmojiGrid`) is untouched and still feeds the copied share text.
- Share block: a "📋 Copy Result" button copies day number, hand, score, Decision Rating, emoji grid, and the player's currently-assembled story text to the clipboard as one block, revealed last in the score reveal sequence.
- Each beat rendered on its own line, not as one run-on paragraph — joined with `\n`, `.story-text` uses `white-space: pre-line`.
- Capped at ~10 options per category (owner request — enough variety without an overwhelming dropdown).
- Humor was intentionally a bit cheekier (owner request: "funny ones that could maybe be seen as slightly inappropriate") — leaning into sassy/self-aware/mildly risqué phrasing rather than anything actually crude.

### 6c. Single-Sentence Caption Builder ✅ built (current design — owner follow-up: "i dont want it to be a story like it was, i want it to be a short phrase")

**Why §6a didn't stick.** §6a's 3-line, comma-joined, fully-nonsense format was a faithful build of the source material's *first* pitch, but after seeing it live the owner wanted something closer to that same source's *other* example — a single flowing sentence, Mad-Libs style, not 3 unrelated lines: *"today i trusted the river like it owed me money 💀."* Re-confirmed the unlock/progression question was still out of scope (owner: "still no unlocks — just a bigger flat pool," consistent with §6a's original scoping call) before touching anything.

**Structure — 6 slots, one sentence, fixed grammatical roles.** `opening` (subject) `action` (past-tense verb) `object` (direct object) `connector` (linking word) `ending` (closing clause) `emoji` (trailing flourish), space-joined into one lowercase line — `${opening} ${action} ${object} ${connector} ${ending} ${emoji}`. Unlike §6a's pools (any phrase could go in any grammatical position), each slot now has a *role* so random combinations mostly parse as an actual sentence even though nothing is really tied together: *"the algorithm betrayed my bluff since i got away with it respectfully."* 22–26 options for opening/action/object, 14 for connector/ending (a naturally smaller category — connectors and clean closing clauses are a tighter set than open-ended nouns/verbs), 22 for emoji.

**`ending` is still the one hand-reactive slot**, same `handStrengthIndex(...) >= handStrengthIndex('TWO_PAIR')` good/bad split as §6a — carried over unchanged, just renamed from `outcome`.

**Grammar bug caught and fixed before shipping, from live spot-checking rather than a formal test:** several `ending` entries were originally written with their own leading "and" (a leftover habit from §6a's comma-joined format, where that read fine) — e.g. "and it paid off," "and here we are." Once `connector` supplies the *only* linking word in the new one-sentence format, an entry starting with its own conjunction collided with any non-"and" connector: *"...unless and here we are"* — a stray double-conjunction. Rewrote every `ending` entry as a bare clause with no leading conjunction of its own (*"and it paid off"* → *"it paid off"*), verified by re-sampling ~15 random hands live rather than just re-running the unit tests (this is a fluency issue, not a structural one — nothing in the test suite could have caught it, since every individual phrase was still independently valid). Also swapped one entry ("against my will") that read fine after some connectors but not others for one that reads cleanly after all of them ("there was no turning back").

**Everything structural from §6a carried forward unchanged**: the player-built dropdown picker (`STORY_SLOT_ORDER` in `board.js` updated to the new slot names — `opening`/`action`/`object`/`connector`/`ending`/`emoji` — nothing else about the picker UI needed to change), the hash-seeded deterministic starting selection with no cross-day memory, the emoji grid and "📋 Copy Result" share block. `describeNotableGroup()`/`{word}` filling stays dropped, same reasoning as §6a.

Verified live via Playwright and by sampling ~15 additional random hands directly through `buildStoryText()`: captions consistently read as one coherent-ish sentence (*"a stranger abandoned my paycheck so i felt unstoppable 🃏"*, *"the house worshipped my paycheck though a dream the math didn't math"*), the picker's 6 dropdowns show the new labels (Opening/Action/Object/Connector/Ending/Emoji), live editing updates the text immediately, Copy Result still works, zero console errors. 254/254 tests passing (rewritten for the single-line assembly and the new slot names).

### 6d. Daily Pool Rotation + Vocabulary Expansion ✅ built (owner request: "i would like for it to not be the same words everyday so there is room for creativity")

**The problem.** Every dropdown was a small, permanently-fixed list — the exact same ~14–26 options forever, every single day. Owner wanted genuine day-to-day freshness, "room for creativity," not the same words on repeat.

**The fix — reuses the exact mechanism the daily hand deal already relies on.** `dailySubset()` (new, `generator.js`) deterministically shuffles a slot's full master vocabulary with a seed seeded from `cardle-captions-<ISO day>-<slot>` (via the same `hashSeed`/`createRng`/`shuffle` trio `deck.js` uses for the actual daily deal) and slices off `DAILY_POOL_SIZE = 16`. Same calendar day → same seed → same 16 options for every player, matching how the daily hand itself already works ("same puzzle for everyone today, different tomorrow"); a new day rolls the seed forward and the visible options change. Seeded **per slot** too (not one shared seed for all 6), so slots don't all rotate in lockstep — `opening` changing doesn't mean `action` changed the same way. A pool smaller than 16 (currently just `connector`, at 14) just shows entirely — nothing to rotate. `getStoryOptions`/`getDefaultSelections`/`buildStoryTextFromSelections`/`buildStoryText`/`generateStory` all gained an optional trailing `date` parameter (defaulting to `new Date()`) to thread this through; `board.js`'s call sites needed no changes since the default covers real usage.

### 7a. Personality Archetypes ✅ built (owner request)

Every completed run is classified into one of 12 archetypes — a different lens on progression from XP: not "how much have you played," but "what kind of player were you *this run*." `src/core/personality.js`, ordered rule list (first match wins, same pattern as the bonus registry), so specificity determines priority:

🦈 The Shark · 🧮 The Statistician · 🔥 The Maniac · 💭 The Dreamer · 🎰 The Gambler · 🌈 The Optimist · ✋ The Hoarder · 💎 The Perfectionist · 🎭 The Wildcard · 🏛️ The Architect · 👻 The Ghost · ⚙️ The Grinder (guaranteed fallback — always matches if nothing more specific does)

The 8 the owner named explicitly (Gambler, Optimist, Shark, Grinder, Hoarder, Dreamer, Statistician, Maniac) are all present; Perfectionist, Wildcard, Architect, and Ghost are the "many more." Classification reads the Meters (§3g), the skill/extra bonuses that fired, and how the final hand compares to the opener — e.g. Shark requires 90%+ Skill *and* a Flush-or-better; Dreamer requires discarding 2+ cards and ending up no better off than the opener. `stats.personalitiesSeen` (§7b) tracks which archetypes a player has been shown across all their runs, feeding two achievements below.

**Bug found and fixed (owner report): Statistician could fire on a 0% Decision Rating.** `Optimal Discard` (and Statistician's threshold on it) scores the *choice* — its EV, decided before the draw — so it can max out even when the draw itself then whiffed to nothing. That's mathematically correct (great decision, bad luck are independent), but labeling a scoreless run "The Statistician" reads as contradictory. Fixed by also requiring `finalHandResult.score > 0`; a perfect-discard-but-total-whiff run now falls through to Dreamer or Ghost instead, both of which fit it better. Regression test added in `tests/personality.test.js`.

**Second bug found and fixed (owner report): "statistician shows up too often, like i just got a decision rating of 27% and still got it."** The real culprit was the threshold, not the score>0 guard above — Statistician required `skillBonuses.optimalDiscard >= 35`, but `optimalDiscardBonus()` (scoring.js) is scored 0-200 as chosenEV's *percentile* among that day's legal discards, so `>= 35` only required beating roughly the bottom sixth of that day's available choices — a merely decent, unremarkable pick, nowhere near "found the mathematically optimal discard" as the label promises. (Decision Rating and `optimalDiscard` measure genuinely different things — outcome-vs-best-EV vs. choice-vs-EV-spread — so a low Decision Rating run passing the old bar isn't itself a contradiction the way the first bug was; the bar was just set far too low to mean "optimal.") Fixed by raising the threshold to `OPTIMAL_DISCARD_MAX_BONUS - 5` (195/200, newly exported from `scoring.js` so this stays anchored to the real max rather than a second hardcoded copy of it) — effectively requiring the literal best (or statistically indistinguishable from it) discard, with a small buffer absorbing any future rounding changes to `optimalDiscardBonus()`. New regression test locks in that a solidly-above-average-but-not-optimal pick (150/200) no longer qualifies.

## 9. Decisions (previously "Open Questions" in v1)

1. **Leaderboard**: two tabs, Score is primary/default; Skill Rating is the secondary tab (§8). ✅
2. **No unlimited mode.** Cardle is exclusively one play per day. This is a deliberate anti-feature: unlimited access would dull the payoff of a good deal and turn a daily ritual into another endless-session game. Every hook in §10 assumes this. ✅
3. **Discard range**: base is up to 3; select modifiers (Fourth Chance, Clean Slate) push it to 4 or 5 for variety (§4). ✅
4. **Backend**: yes — **Supabase** (§11). Rationale: Postgres gives clean `ORDER BY score` / `ORDER BY decision_rating` leaderboard queries out of the box (a NoSQL store like Firestore would fight this), built-in anonymous auth means players can appear on a leaderboard with zero signup friction, generous free tier, and the client SDK is a plain `<script>`/ESM import — no server code to run or host, which fits "no frameworks unless there's a compelling reason" for a solo-maintained project. ✅

The reason is structural and specific to this game: HAND_RANKS is deliberately odds-proportional (score ∝ 1/probability, §3s), which by construction makes every hand category contribute roughly equally to the expected value — so the EV depends on rare-but-enormous categories, and one extra sampled quad swings the estimate wildly. Recorded in ev-solver.js so it isn't re-attempted.
    - **Kept the math exact, removed the allocations.** `combinationIndices` yielded a fresh `.slice()` per combination (up to 1.5M throwaway arrays per solve) and `evForDiscard` rebuilt the candidate hand each iteration; both now reuse one buffer, with the single caller that outlives the loop copying explicitly.
    - **Pruned the wild search, provably without changing results.** `evaluateFixedHand` only ever reads suit via `isFlush`, so unless every other card already shares one suit a flush is impossible and the wild's suit cannot affect the category or score — 13 substitutions instead of 52. Behavior-preserving rather than approximate: the search keeps a candidate only on a strictly greater score, so when suit is irrelevant the first suit tried already won every tie. Pinned by a test that brute-forces all 52 and asserts equality.
    - **Moved the solve off the main thread** (`src/core/solve-worker.js` + `src/state/solver.js`, in state/ because it needs the browser `Worker` API that core/ is kept free of). Falls back to solving inline whenever a worker is unavailable or errors — i.e. worst case is the old behavior. Net result: worst case 15.4s → 223ms of *work*, and the worst main-thread stall during the heaviest possible solve measured at **11ms** (verified the worker is genuinely fetched and used, not silently falling back). board.js shows a "Crunching the odds…" state that can now actually animate.

    **Scoring correctness (four bugs).**
    - **A "Busted — Nothing" run paid 270 points.** `pityBonus` wasn't clamped for negative input, and scoreRun legitimately passes one (`multipliedTotal - cardValue.total`, negative when Double or Nothing's bust zeroes the total while Card Value stays 50-350) — so the formula ran past its own documented maximum: `pityBonus(-350)` returned 360. Input clamped at 0, making PITY_MAX_BONUS an actual maximum again.
    - **Flush Frenzy paid nothing on a Straight Flush or Royal Flush.** The check was `id === 'FLUSH'` exactly, on a day whose own text reads "Flushes score 4x today" — landing the rarest hand in poker was worth less than an ordinary flush. Now matches all three flush categories.
    - **Suit Bonus counted a Wild by its meaningless dealt suit** — the exact bug §3t fixed for Suit Synergy, reintroduced through a different door: scoreRun invoked the modifier callback with the *raw* hand, before `logicalFinalHand` existed. A Wild dealt as 2♥ but playing as a spade to complete a spade flush scored as a heart on a hearts-bonus day. The callback now receives the logical hand (and its JSDoc says so).
    - **A second Wild in one hand wasn't wild.** `evaluateHand` used `findIndex`, so only the first was ever substituted; any second kept its junk dealt rank/suit. Two Wilds plus 9♠ J♠ K♠ evaluated as a Three Straight (613) when the real best is a Straight Flush (6,080,000) — now found correctly (10♠ + Q♠). Exhaustive for one or two Wilds (the only counts reachable in real play); three or more, only possible via the admin hand builder, resolve greedily one at a time rather than hanging on a 52ⁿ search. `wildSubstitutions` exposes them all by position; the old single-`wildSubstitution` field still works for every existing caller.

    **Security — stored XSS through usernames.** `schema.sql`'s username CHECK was **length-only**, so the `^[A-Za-z0-9_]{3,20}$` pattern existed *only* in client JavaScript. Since Supabase exposes these tables straight to the browser and the anon key is public by design, anyone could call the REST API directly and store `<svg onload=...>` — 14 characters, comfortably inside the limit — and `header.js` rendered other people's usernames into the friends list via `innerHTML`, executing it in the viewer's browser with their session. Confirmed executing in Chromium before the fix. Fixed on both sides, deliberately: a Postgres regex constraint keeps the value out of the database (`supabase/migrations/001-username-pattern-constraint.sql`, idempotent, with a pre-flight query for existing violations, since the table is already deployed), and `header.js`/`modal.js` escape on output so even a value that somehow gets in renders as inert text. Re-verified with a malicious username served from a mocked backend: no execution, no injected element, name displayed as literal text. **Migration confirmed applied to the live project by the owner** — both halves of this fix are in force.

    **Two dead features.** `meters.js` normalized Risk's "chase" component against ROYAL_FLUSH (~55 million) — a leftover from before HAND_RANKS was rescaled odds-proportionally, when the top of the table was ~5,000. At realistic scores that term contributed **under one point** of its 40 (Two Pair: 0.001), so Risk was just `discardedCount/maxDiscards × 60`, capped at 60 — which in turn made **"The Gambler"** (needs `risk >= 65`) reachable only by being dealt a Royal Flush and throwing it away: 1 of 12 personalities nobody would ever see. Re-anchored to THREE_OF_A_KIND, so abandoning a real made hand now reads as a genuine gamble and The Gambler is selectable in ordinary play (verified end to end through `classifyPersonality`). The meters test's own fixture comment had been written *around* this bug ("Full House alone rounds away to nothing") and is updated.

    **One regression of my own, from §49's Draw button.** `.card--deal-in` is declared `animation-fill-mode: both`, and a filled animation's `transform` outranks any class rule's in the CSS cascade — so the class pinned each card at the animation's final `translateY(0) scale(1)` and silently overrode `.card--flip-out`'s `rotateY(90deg)`. The opening reveal became a hard cut with no flip at all, while the discard-replacement flip still worked, which is why it wasn't obvious. Caught by sampling the computed transform mid-flip (the identity matrix, not a rotation). Fixed by dropping the class on `animationend`; re-sampled after the fix and the transform is now a real progressing 3D rotation.

    Test suite grew 307 → 317, all passing. Its runtime dropped from ~3.8s to ~1.3s as a side effect of the solver work, then rose back to ~3.6s — but only because of a newly *added* worst-case guard (the 5-discard Clean Slate solve, 2.3s on its own) that deliberately exercises the heaviest thing the game can ask for; the pre-existing tests are the ones that got faster.
55. **Poem editing behind an "Edit Poem" button, Copy Result always present** — owner: "i want to always have a copy result button on the bottom. i would also like to rework the poem system so that it gives a random starting poem like it always has done but instead of having all the options immediately, have an 'edit poem' button that then drops down the selections and a submit button after." This partly *reverses* §50: there, Submit occupied the Copy Result slot and Copy Result only appeared once you'd submitted. The problem with that arrangement was that the poem is already complete the moment it renders (it's a seeded random selection — §6c), so gating Copy behind Submit made the player confirm something that was never unfinished. Now:
    - Default state is the finished poem plus one bottom row: `[✏️ Edit Poem] [📋 Copy Result]`. The six slot pickers are collapsed, so an ordinary player never sees a wall of dropdowns.
    - Edit Poem reveals the pickers with a Submit button after them, and hides itself. Submit only *closes* the editor — every dropdown change already applies to the poem live, so there's nothing to commit; it exists to get the pickers back out of the way. Reopening works repeatedly.
    - Copy Result is always visible AND always the bottom-most element. The editor is deliberately placed above the button row in the DOM rather than below it, so opening the pickers can't push Copy Result up into the middle of the block — verified by asserting its measured `getBoundingClientRect().bottom` exceeds both the editor's and Submit's while editing, rather than just eyeballing it.
    - `.story-editor` deliberately declares no `display` property, with a comment saying why: a `block`/`flex` value there would override the browser's default `[hidden] { display: none }` and stop the editor collapsing at all — the same trap that had already hit `.draw-btn`, `.story-builder`, `.meters`, `.personality-badge` and `.login-form` (§50/§54).
    Verified with 22 browser assertions across both render paths (the animated reveal and the "already played today" reload, which share `renderStoryBlock`) plus a 375px mobile pass confirming no horizontal overflow in either state.

## 10. Art Direction — cards

Flat, geometric, high-contrast, low-ornamentation:
- Custom SVG suit glyphs, 1–2px stroke weight, no gloss/gradient/drop-shadow "3D render" look.
- Two-tone rank/suit coloring per theme accent (§5) on the card *back* only — faces stay neutral/high-contrast so themes never hurt readability.
- Generous whitespace, clean pip grid layout — closer to a modern Swiss-design deck or Balatro's flat sprite style than a photorealistic casino deck.
- Same visual language extends to the emoji share grid and UI chrome (buttons, modals) for a coherent, non-generic feel.
- **No sound** (§9.6) — feedback is entirely visual/motion: a cascading card-flip reveals replacement cards, and the score builds up line-by-line, lowest to highest (§3j), rather than appearing instantly. Confetti-for-rare-hands from GAME_PLAN.md is still open (not yet built) — though the rare-card glow pulse (§3h) now covers a chunk of that same "make it feel special" goal.
- **Rare cards break the "flat, no gloss" rule on purpose** (§3h) — a colored border/glow scaling with rarity tier, and a slower flip + pulse animation on reveal. Deliberate exception: the whole point is that these read as unmistakably different from the otherwise-uniform deck.

## 11. Technical Architecture

```
/core
  deck.js            — seeded RNG, deck construction/shuffle (daily seed only — no arbitrary/unlimited seeding); also rolls each card's rarity (§3h)
  rarity.js           — RARITIES tier list: chance + bonus points + emoji/label, single source of truth (§3h)
  hand-evaluator.js   — pure function: 5 cards -> {rank, score}; wild-Joker substitution search when a 'joker'-rarity card is present (§3h)
  ev-solver.js        — optimal-discard EV computation (Decision Rating + Optimal Discard bonus)
  scoring.js           — base + flavor + suit synergy + skill + extra-bonus-registry + rarity (flat bonus + hand-synergy multiplier, §3m) + pity + modifier bonuses
  bonus-registry.js    — the RNGDLE-style stacking bonus list (§3f)
  meters.js            — Luck / Skill / Risk (§3g)
  personality.js       — run -> archetype classifier (§7a)
  achievements.js      — achievement registry (§7b)
/modifiers
  registry.js          — modifier objects w/ lifecycle hooks: onDeal, onScoreTable, onScore, onDisplay
  (one file per modifier)
/themes
  registry.js          — theme -> {palette, compatible modifiers, story vocabulary}
/story
  templates.js          — FRAGMENTS (structural + flavor phrase pools) + SLOT_META (§6)
  generator.js           — assembles fragments into story text, builds the emoji grid + share text
  vocabulary.js           — rank/suit flavor words
/state
  daily-seed.js        — date -> deterministic seed
  persistence.js        — localStorage: today's full result (score, meters, personality, unlocked achievements)
  stats.js               — localStorage: cumulative history (games played, hands/personalities seen, unlocked achievements) — feeds §7b's cumulative achievements
  test-mode.js            — ?test query param handling; never touches persistence.js or stats.js
  (no cross-day story-preference store — removed per owner request, §6)
/backend (Supabase)
  - anonymous auth on first visit (upgradeable to named profile for leaderboard display)
  - tables: daily_runs(user_id, date, score, decision_rating, modifier_id, theme_id), profiles(user_id, display_name, streak, freeze_tokens, xp), achievements(user_id, achievement_id, unlocked_at)
  - RLS: a user may only insert their own row for *today's* date — prevents replay/backdating
  - leaderboard = simple ranked query per day; skill rating = rolling aggregate over daily_runs
/ui
  cards, animations, board, modals
```

Streamlined vs. v1: no unlimited-mode seed generator, no separate practice-XP track — everything funnels through the one daily run.

### 11b. Duplicate Username-Prompt Modal ✅ fixed (owner bug report: "when someone successfully logs in for the first time it prompts them to make a username, and when they chose a username it prompts for it again, and it says it is taken")

**Root cause**: Supabase's `onAuthStateChange` fires more than once for a single sign-in (once restoring `INITIAL_SESSION`, again for the actual `SIGNED_IN` event) — `header.js`'s `refreshProfile()` ran on every firing and, seeing no `profiles` row yet on *either* firing (the first `createProfile()` call hadn't necessarily resolved before the second firing checked), opened a second username-prompt modal stacked on top of the first. Submitting through the stale second modal — after the first had already created the profile — hit `profiles.username`'s unique constraint: "taken" by the user's own, just-created account.

**The fix**: a `usernamePromptOpenForUserId` guard in `header.js`, set the instant a prompt opens and checked before opening another; `modal.js` gained an `onClose` callback (fires no matter which of the four ways a modal closes — × button, backdrop click, Escape, or the render callback's own `close()`) so the guard reliably clears even if the user dismisses the prompt without submitting.

**Verified with a controlled reproduction**, since this depends on real Supabase timing that can't be forced from the outside normally: intercepted the `esm.sh` Supabase SDK import in Playwright with a fake client whose `onAuthStateChange` fires twice (mirroring the real quirk) and whose `insert()` simulates a real unique-violation on a repeat username. Before the fix this would have opened 2 modals; after, only 1 opens despite the double firing, and submitting through it succeeds cleanly (1 insert call, no "taken" error, modal closes, header shows the new username). Zero console errors. No unit-test changes (this is DOM/timing-only, same precedent as the rest of the animation/UI layer). Owner confirmed fixed on localhost before this was pushed.

### 11d. Profile Page, XP & Levels ✅ built (owner: "make a full profile page, once you click on the username top right it takes you to your profile with a whole bunch of stats, your 5 recent hands (with a load more to show more) your highest hand, total points, and this is where you can sign out or delete account at the bottom... i also want a full xp and level system")

Pass 1 of a two-pass build (see decision log #56 for why it was split; pass 2 is the cosmetics layer — badges, titles, name paints).

**Everything is DERIVED from `daily_plays`, with no new stats table.** Every row already stores the complete run result (`score`, `finalHand`, `personalityId`, `decisionRating`, `dayNumber`), so hands played, total points, best score, highest hand, streaks, averages, hand/personality collections, achievements and total XP are all recomputed from rows that already existed. `src/state/stats.js`'s localStorage tally is left alone but is no longer what a profile reads: it's per-browser, so it can't back an account-level profile, doesn't survive clearing site data, and any bug in its incremental update path would corrupt the total permanently. Recomputation is correct on every device, self-heals after a bad write, and has exactly one source of truth. `core/player-stats.js` (pure) does the derivation; `state/profile.js` does the fetching.

**XP is its own scale, deliberately not a function of points.** `HAND_RANKS` is odds-proportional (§3s), so a Royal Flush is worth 54,800,000 against a Pair's 200 — a level curve driven by raw points would jump one lucky player hundreds of levels ahead of everyone and make an ordinary day feel worthless. Instead (`core/progression.js`): a flat base per run (so a bad day never stalls progression), plus hand category, plus Decision Rating clamped to 0-1 (the one skill term — the excess on a lucky draw is luck, which the category term already pays for), plus small per-bonus and per-achievement amounts. Levels use a triangular curve (`XP_PER_LEVEL_STEP * (level-1) * level / 2`) so early levels land every run or two while level 20 takes most of a year — appropriate when there's exactly one run per day (§9.2). `levelForXp` is the closed-form inverse, so it stays O(1) however far a player progresses. A test asserts the best hand is worth under 5x the worst in XP, pinning the whole point of the separate scale.

**Highest hand means the best CATEGORY, not the biggest score** — rank-scaling plus bonuses means a Pair can out-score a genuine Straight, and a player asking for their highest hand means the poker hand. Best score is tracked separately alongside it.

**Achievements are the union of what was recorded at the time and a fresh replay.** Each run's stored `newlyUnlocked` is unioned with re-evaluating `evaluateAchievements()` against cumulative stats rebuilt run-by-run in play order. The union matters in both directions: replay alone would silently drop anything whose rule has since changed, and the stored list alone would miss cumulative achievements for runs played before this existed (those were only ever evaluated against per-browser localStorage stats). `board.js` now also records `maxDiscards` in the result, since two achievement rules compare against the day's cap and it wasn't otherwise recoverable; older rows leave it undefined, which those rules treat as "not satisfied" rather than unlocking something unearned.

**Routing is `?profile`, not `/profile`.** This is a static single-page app with no router; a real path would need a Vercel rewrite plus matching dev-server handling — the exact area that produced the "unstyled page" deployment bug (§11a). A query param needs zero server config and behaves identically locally and deployed. It's shaped to extend to `?profile=<username>` for viewing a friend's profile later without changing how routing works. Clicking your own username in the header navigates there, which replaces the small sign-out-only modal that used to open — signing out now lives on the profile, as requested.

**Delete account needs a Postgres function, not client-side deletes.** The browser holds only the public anon key, which deliberately cannot touch `auth.users`; deleting just the app's own tables from the client would leave a stranded auth user able to sign in to a half-deleted account. `delete_own_account()` (`supabase/migrations/002-delete-own-account.sql`) runs `security definer` so it *can* remove the auth row, and is safe because it takes no arguments and is hard-scoped to `auth.uid()` — there is no parameter to tamper with, so a caller can only ever delete their own account. `search_path` is pinned so a caller can't shadow `auth`/`public`, EXECUTE is granted only to `authenticated`, and an unauthenticated call raises rather than silently no-opping. Everything else (`profiles`, `daily_plays`, `friendships`) cascades from that one delete. The UI requires typing your username to confirm — the one action in the app that can't be undone.

**Bounded history read.** `fetchPlayHistory` caps at 400 rows (over a year of perfect attendance) because each `result` is a few KB of JSON and an unbounded history would eventually be a slow mobile download for no visible benefit. Rows with a null `result` — a claimed-but-unfinished seed (§11c) — are excluded, so they can't count toward games played, streaks, or averages.

**Verified** with 39 new unit tests (`progression.test.js`, `player-stats.test.js`) covering the XP/level maths, streak edge cases (gaps, lapses, duplicate dates, month boundaries, unparseable dates), the category-vs-score distinction, achievement replay, and malformed/legacy rows; plus 46 browser assertions against a mocked backend covering the full render, load-more paging, the rare/wild card rings, the delete confirmation gate (disabled until the username matches, RPC only fired after), a failing delete surfacing an error without navigating, the signed-out and zero-history states, header navigation, and no horizontal overflow at 375px.

### 11e. Verified by measuring actual `getBoundingClientRect()` positions (title below the name, badge left of it) rather than eyeballing a screenshot.

**One shared renderer.** `ui/nameplate.js` is the single place deciding how badge/title/paint combine, used by the site header, the profile page preview, and the friends list — so a nameplate looks identical everywhere. Every dynamic value is escaped, and the paint is applied as a class from a fixed registry rather than an inline style, so a stored id can never inject markup or CSS.

**Validation is split between database and app, deliberately.** `profiles` gained `equipped_badge`/`equipped_title`/`equipped_paint` (migration 003), each CHECK-constrained to `^[A-Za-z0-9_]{1,40}$`. That validates the id SHAPE — these render next to a username *in other people's friends lists*, so the same lesson as migration 001 applies: the app's rules must exist in the database too, since the anon key lets anyone call the REST API directly. A pattern rather than a hardcoded id list, so adding a cosmetic needs no migration; `resolveEquipped()` resolves anything unrecognized to nothing, so an id outside the registries simply does not render. Unlock status is **not** enforced in SQL: doing so would mean replaying achievements and recomputing XP in Postgres — duplicating `core/player-stats.js`'s whole derivation, kept in sync by hand, for a purely cosmetic stake. The worst a determined user gets by writing to their own row is displaying a badge they did not earn; RLS still prevents touching anyone else's data, their score, or their level. `canEquip()` gates the picker and is re-checked before saving, so a page left open across a level-up cannot persist something unearned.

**Saves are optimistic with a revert.** Selecting a chip updates the preview immediately, then persists; a failed save rolls the selection back and says why, rather than leaving the UI claiming something that is not stored. The default paint is written as `null` rather than its id — "nothing chosen" and "explicitly chose the default" are the same thing, and null keeps the column empty for the common case.

### 11f. Admin Page ✅ pass 1 built (owner: "i want an admin page just for one account 'car' which is me to where only i can access it, i want to be able to control everything from it")

Pass 1 of two: the security foundation plus player administration. Pass 2 is server-backed daily config (overriding the modifier, editing the poem word bank, authoring brand-new cosmetics).

**THE CENTRAL POINT: "only I can access it" cannot be delivered in the browser.** Cardle is a static site. Every line of its JavaScript is public and the Supabase key it ships is the public anon key, so a check like `if (username === 'car')` is decoration — anyone can read the bundle, see it, and call the same REST endpoints without it. Building the page that way would have *looked* secure while being trivially bypassable, which is worse than not shipping it.

So the boundary lives in Postgres (`supabase/migrations/004-admin-foundation.sql`):
- `public.is_admin()` is `security definer`, takes **no arguments**, and reads only `auth.uid()`'s own row. There is no parameter to tamper with.
- Every privileged operation (`admin_overview`, `admin_set_cosmetics`, `admin_set_unlocks`, `admin_reset_day`, `admin_delete_player`) is `security definer` and its **first statement** re-checks `is_admin()`, because the caller is untrusted by definition. `search_path` is pinned on each; EXECUTE is granted to `authenticated` only, never `anon`.
- Admins additionally get a SELECT policy on `daily_plays` (an extra permissive policy, so ordinary players are unchanged).
- The client-side check decides only what to **render**. Verified by patching the gate away entirely in a browser and calling the data-layer functions directly as a non-admin: overview, granting unlocks, and deleting a player were all refused by the "database", not the page.

**A privilege-escalation hole had to be closed for the flag to be safe at all.** `profiles` had an UPDATE policy of `auth.uid() = id` with **no column restriction** — a user could already write any column on their own row via a direct REST call. Adding `is_admin` under that policy would have let *any signed-in user grant themselves admin*: the flag would have been its own exploit. Fixed by revoking blanket UPDATE from `authenticated` and re-granting it only on `username`/`equipped_badge`/`equipped_title`/`equipped_paint`. Exactly the same class of bug, and the same fix, as `daily_plays.seed` (§54) — which is the second time this pattern has bitten, so it's worth stating as a rule: **on this project, an RLS row policy is never sufficient on its own; every table with a privileged column needs a matching column grant.**

**Admin identity is data, not code** (owner's choice): the migration adds `is_admin` and the owner runs one line to set it. Nothing about who is an admin appears in the public bundle, it survives a username change, and a second admin needs no deploy. `admin_set_cosmetics` deliberately cannot touch `is_admin` — promoting an admin stays a SQL-console action, so a compromised admin session can't mint more admins. `admin_delete_player` refuses to delete an admin (including yourself), since that click is unrecoverable and could lock the project out of its own admin access.

**Granting cosmetics needed new storage, because unlocks are derived.** Normal unlocks come from achievements and level (§11e), computed from history — so there was nowhere to record "an admin gave this player that badge", and a grant would have been silently re-locked on the next recompute. `profiles.admin_unlocks text[]` (admin-writable only) force-unlocks specific ids; `resolveCosmetics` unions it in and flags entries as `grantedByAdmin`. Additive only — it never re-locks something legitimately earned, which a test pins. Ids are shape-checked in SQL for the same reason as the equipped columns: they render next to usernames in other people's browsers.

**Caught while verifying: the grant would have done nothing.** `profile.js` computed the player's own picker from level + achievements only, so an admin-granted cosmetic showed as locked to the very player who received it. Fixed by threading `admin_unlocks` through, via a single shared `playerContext` used by both the picker and the pre-save `canEquip` re-check so the two can't drift apart.

**"Active players" is derived from play data** (owner's choice of the 7-day definition, shown alongside 24h and 30d for trend). Real login timestamps live in `auth.users` and aren't readable from the browser, so completed runs are the honest available signal — the page says so rather than implying it tracks visits. All counters are computed in SQL by `admin_overview()` so the payload stays constant as the tables grow.

**The modifier schedule is read-only in this pass, and labelled as such.** The next 7 days are shown (with today highlighted) because that's genuinely derivable today — `getDailyModifier()` is a pure function of the date. *Changing* it, and editing the poem word bank, require introducing a server-side config table that clients read at boot, which is a real architectural change to code that is deliberately pure right now. Shipping a disabled-looking control would have been dishonest, so the section states plainly that it can't be edited yet.

**Verified** with 7 new unit tests for admin grants (additive-only, `grantedByAdmin` flagging, unknown ids unlocking nothing, `canEquip` honouring grants) plus 43 browser assertions: non-admins see no link and are refused at `?admin`; the database refuses even with the client gate removed; overview counters; the 7-day schedule with today highlighted; search; the player panel with derived stats; equipping on someone's behalf; toggling grants on and off; earned cosmetics shown but not grantable; day reset actually reducing their run count; the type-the-name delete gate; admin accounts protected from deletion; and no overflow at 375px.

### 11g. Server-Side Game Config ✅ built (owner: "change the daily modifier and see what day is next and be able to change that... edit the word bank of the poems from there... add customizations to the profile like new badges and paints and titles")

Pass 2 of the admin build (pass 1 is §11f). This is the pass that made the previously read-only parts actually editable.

**The problem it solves.** The daily modifier, the poem word bank, and the cosmetic registries were all pure client-side constants — a function of the date, and hardcoded arrays. Fast and testable, but unchangeable without a deploy, which is why §11f could only *show* the modifier schedule. `game_config` (migration 005) is a small key/value jsonb table: publicly readable so every player's client honours the same rules, admin-writable only.

**core/ stays pure.** Nothing in `src/core/` was given network access. Instead the config is fetched by `src/state/game-config.js` and PASSED IN as ordinary arguments:
- `getDailyModifier(date, overrideId)` — an optional second argument.
- Every fragment-reading function in `story/generator.js` takes `fragments` as its LAST argument, defaulting to the built-in `FRAGMENTS`, so all existing call sites keep working untouched.
- `resolveCosmetics`/`resolveEquipped` take a `custom` registry.
That keeps the whole rule set expressible and unit-testable without a database, which is what made 36 new tests possible for logic that ultimately depends on a remote table.

**Writes go through exactly one authorized path.** `game_config` has RLS enabled and a SELECT policy but **no** INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE policy at all, so a direct REST write is refused even for an admin. The only way in is `admin_set_config()`/`admin_clear_config()`, both `security definer` and both re-checking `is_admin()`. Clearing a key *deletes the row* rather than storing an empty value, because absent ("use the built-in default") and empty ("an admin deliberately cleared this") are genuinely different states.

**Validated in both directions, and the outward direction matters more.** `core/game-config.js` holds pure validators used on the way IN (the admin page refuses to save something broken) and again on the way OUT (`loadGameConfig` re-validates every row it reads). The second half is the important one: this table is read by *every* client on boot, so one malformed row could otherwise break the game for everyone. A bad value degrades to the built-in default. Partial validity is preserved too — one invalid date in the override map doesn't discard the valid entries.

**Availability was the design constraint.** `loadGameConfig()` never rejects; it resolves to built-in defaults. A blocked CDN, an offline player, an unrun migration, or a Supabase outage all leave the game fully playable with the shipped rules — verified by a test that plays a complete run with all network blocked and confirms the poem falls back to the built-in word bank. Same lesson as the top-level-await CDN failure (§54): one optional backend read must never be able to take the whole game down.

**Word bank edits are PARTIAL by design.** Only slots that actually differ from their built-in default are stored. An admin editing the emoji list shouldn't have to re-enter all six slots, and — more importantly — a slot they never touched keeps receiving future built-in additions instead of being frozen at whatever shipped the day they saved. Editing is one phrase per line, which matches how the fragments read; the validator enforces the same single-line invariant templates.js's own tests enforce for the built-ins (no internal periods), and trims/de-duplicates automatically.

**Custom paints are a solid hex colour only, and that's a security decision rather than a shortcut.** A built-in paint is a CSS class, which cannot be authored at runtime; a custom one therefore carries a colour applied as an inline `style`. Since a nameplate renders in *other players'* browsers, the validator demands a strict 6-digit hex — a test drives `red`, `#fff`, `rgb(0,0,0)`, `url(x)` and `#000000;background:red` through it and asserts every one is refused. Gradients and animation stay code-only because they'd need real CSS. Custom titles and paints unlock by level like the built-ins; a custom **badge** has no achievement behind it, so it is grant-only (§11f's `admin_unlocks`) and the UI says so rather than showing an unreachable requirement. Custom entries are APPENDED — a custom id can never shadow or replace a built-in.

**Caught and fixed: I regressed the Draw button's responsiveness.** My first cut awaited the config fetch *before revealing* the Draw button, to guarantee an override was in force before dealing. Measured, that pushed the button from ~117ms to ~960ms on a real connection. The actual requirement is only that the override applies before CARDS ARE DEALT — a hand dealt under the wrong discard cap is unrecoverable for that player's day — so the await moved into the click handler. Back to ~126ms, with the guarantee intact and verified: a test pins today's modifier to Clean Slate and confirms a player's board shows it *and* that the button reads "Discard 0/5" before any card is dealt.

**Verified** with 36 new unit tests (override validation and fallback, `getDailyModifier` ignoring unknown ids while keeping a day-stable random context, word-bank merge semantics including the merged pools actually driving a generated caption, custom-cosmetic validation including the colour-injection guard, and custom entries not shadowing built-ins) plus 40 browser assertions: the 14-day editable schedule, saving and clearing overrides, a player's board honouring a pinned modifier and its discard cap, the word-bank editor round-tripping into a real player's poem, validation refusing a phrase with a period, authoring/removing custom cosmetics, a custom paint reaching a player's picker and computing the right colour, an invalid id refused before saving, a non-admin refused server-side even with the client gate removed, and a full playthrough with the backend entirely unavailable.

### 11h. Cosmetic Availability Modes ✅ built (owner bug report: "add an option when adding titles, badges and paints to have it unlocked for everyone or locked/hidden. i just added an owner title and now everyone has it")

**A real design flaw in §11g, not a misuse.** Custom titles and paints were level-gated only, so the level field was the sole way to express availability — and since every player is at least level 1, an "Owner" title authored at level 1 unlocked for the entire playerbase. There was no way to say "this one is mine" or "this one is simply for everybody". The owner hit this immediately on the first real use.

**Three modes**, chosen to match the two the owner asked for plus the existing behaviour:
- `level` — unlocks at a level threshold. The original behaviour, and still the default for titles and paints so nothing already authored changes meaning.
- `everyone` — unlocked for every player immediately, level ignored.
- `grant` — obtainable only via an admin grant (§11f's `admin_unlocks`), **and hidden** from a player's picker unless they actually have it. The owner said "locked/hidden"; for an exclusive cosmetic those are the same thing — advertising a title nobody can earn is worse than not showing it.

A custom **badge** cannot be level-gated at all (it has no achievement behind it), so `level` collapses to `grant` for badges rather than silently creating something unreachable. Absent availability defaults per kind — `grant` for badges, `level` for titles and paints — so cosmetics authored before this existed keep behaving exactly as they did.

**Re-locking has to actually take the cosmetic away, and that was the hard part.** Flipping an over-permissive cosmetic to `grant` is worthless if everyone who already equipped it keeps wearing it — the accidental Owner title could never be recalled. So `resolveEquipped()` now drops a `grant`-mode custom cosmetic unless the profile row carries the grant.

That's deliberately scoped to `grant` mode, and the limit is worth recording: ownership for `grant` mode is exactly `admin_unlocks`, which lives **on the profile row**, so it can be judged anywhere a nameplate renders — including a friend's row, where no play history is available. Level- and achievement-gated ownership would need the player's whole history, which a friends list doesn't have. `ownsGrantOnly()` answers only the narrow question it can answer definitively, and says so.

**The admin panel deliberately ignores `hidden`** — it has to list a hidden cosmetic in order to grant it. Only the player-facing picker filters on it.

**Also fixed a naming smell this exposed.** The custom-cosmetic "Remove" button reused `.admin-select-btn`, whose name means "select a player". A browser test clicked Remove when it meant Manage, which is exactly the confusion the shared name invites; it now has its own `.admin-remove-btn` class sharing the same styling.

**Verified** with 14 new unit tests and a 15-assertion browser suite that reproduces the reported bug before fixing it: author "Owner" at level 1, confirm an ordinary player can see AND equip it, re-author as locked/hidden, then confirm it disappears from their picker *and* stops rendering on the nameplate they'd already equipped it to — while the admin can still see and grant it, a granted player keeps it, and an `everyone` paint reaches a level-1 player.

### 11i. Fix Batch — Login Flash, Username Collision, Sent Invites, Tab Icon, Run Grade ✅ built

### 11j. Leaderboards + Viewable Player Profiles ✅ built (owner: "leaderboards... be able to click friends profile")

The last two items of the punch-list, built together because they need the same thing: permission to read other players' scores.

**One migration serves both.** `daily_plays`' SELECT policy was own-rows-only, which blocked a leaderboard AND blocked showing a friend's history. Asked how open it should be; the owner chose completed runs readable by anyone, specifically because friends-only would have forced the boards to be friends-only too.

**The policy is written narrowly, and the narrowness is the point.** It grants `to authenticated` where **`result is not null`** — not a blanket `using (true)`. A row with a null result carries the SEED for a hand the player hasn't locked in yet (§11c), so a blanket policy would have let anyone read another player's seed, deal it themselves, and see today's hand before them. That's the one genuinely sensitive thing in the table, so the policy is shaped around it.

**Four boards** (owner's spec): Today, This Week, All-Time top scores, and All-Time career points, each with a friends-only toggle.
- The three top-score boards are **one** SQL function with a different date window, not three — the only difference is a cutoff, and three copies would mean fixing any future bug three times.
- Computed in SQL so the payload stays a fixed handful of rows however many players exist, and the score is dug out of the jsonb result (`result->'score'->>'total'`) in one place. Cast to `numeric` rather than `bigint` because totals reach into the hundreds of millions once multipliers stack, and a malformed row could hold anything.
- `security invoker` (the default), deliberately **not** `security definer`: they read exactly what the caller may already read, so there's no reason to elevate — and not elevating means a future policy change automatically applies here too.
- A partial index on `play_date` where `result is not null`, since every board sorts on it.

**Friends-only filtering happens client-side, on purpose.** Friendship rows are already readable only to the two people involved (§11a), so the client can cheaply fetch its own friend ids and filter — whereas doing it in SQL would mean threading an id list through every board or duplicating the friendship join inside each function. The cost is asking for a bigger page and trimming it, which is why the friends path requests 100 rows for a 25-row board.

**Your own row is highlighted** wherever you land, which is the main reason to look at a board you aren't winning.

**Viewing another player reuses the profile page rather than duplicating it.** `?profile=<username>` — the shape §11d was already designed for — resolves the username to an id and renders the same page with `canEdit` false, which hides the Customize and Account sections and skips wiring their handlers. Everything else (derived stats, history, achievements, collection, nameplate) is identical, so a friend's profile can't drift out of sync with your own. Names are clickable from both the leaderboard and the friends list.

Three edge cases are handled explicitly and tested: visiting **your own** username by URL gives the editable page rather than a read-only copy of it; an unknown username says so rather than rendering an empty profile; and a signed-out visitor is told to log in, because reading anyone's runs requires an authenticated caller — a policy fact, not a UI preference.

**A real regression, caught by three existing suites.** The new header button pushed the right-hand controls (Ranks, name, Friends, Admin) past a 375px viewport. Fixed by letting that group wrap and trimming the button labels to their icons below 460px — in CSS rather than JS, so it responds to rotation and resize with no listener. Verified at 375px and 900px.

### 11l. Game Day at 7pm, Leaderboard Hands & Grade Colours ✅ built (owner: "rename the ranks tab to 'Leaderboards', have the hand the player won with on the leaderboards, and also make the score rarities pop more with better highlighted colors on them. also add a timer to the next draw reset, it should happen at 7pm est")

Four items. Three are small; the reset time quietly touches everything that asks "what day is it?".

**"7pm EST" needed one clarifying question.** Taken literally, EST is a fixed −05:00 offset, so a year-round EST reset would drift to 8pm local through the summer. Offered the choice; the owner picked **7pm New York time year-round**, which is what "7pm" means to a player — so the boundary follows DST.

**The reset is a single pure module, and the whole app now goes through it.** `src/core/game-day.js` owns `RESET_HOUR_NY`, `gameDayFor`, `nextResetAt`, `msUntilNextReset`, `resetCountdown`, `formatCountdown` and `gameDayNumber`. It uses `Intl.DateTimeFormat` with a `timeZone`, so DST is handled by the platform's own tz database rather than by a hand-written offset table — no dependency, and correct across future DST rule changes. Converting a New York wall-clock time back to UTC iterates to convergence, because the offset you need depends on the instant you're computing.

Before this, "today" was `new Date().toISOString().slice(0,10)` open-coded in seven places (persistence, the daily-play record, modifiers, the caption generator, streaks, profile, admin). All seven now call `gameDayFor`. That consolidation was the actual work — with the boundary no longer at midnight UTC, any straggler would have disagreed with the rest about which puzzle is live.

**No historical data migration was needed, and that is a fact worth having verified rather than assumed.** In winter, 19:00 EST *is* midnight UTC, so labelling a game day as "New York local date, +1 if the local hour is ≥ 19" produces byte-identical strings to the old UTC-calendar-day rule. Only the 23:00–00:00 UTC hour during EDT shifts. A test pins this property explicitly, because it is the entire reason existing `play_date` values could be left alone.

**The database had to agree.** A SQL `game_today()` mirrors `gameDayFor` clause for clause — take `now() at time zone 'America/New_York'`, and add a day once its hour reaches 19 — and `admin_overview`, `admin_reset_day` and the leaderboard windows all use it instead of `current_date`. ### 11n. Uppercase Usernames, Reveal Order, Leaderboard Polish & Profile Hero ✅ built (owner: "change all names to all caps and all future names to be all caps... align hands on the leaderboard... make the score on the leaderboards be highlighted in the rarity of the score instead of just showing the rarity next to it... add animated name paints and point rarity highlights... fix when discarding a card, and the next card draw is a rare card it sometimes reveals out of order... the words in the header dont look centered in their little circles... make the profile more inviting")

Six items. The reveal-order bug and the header centring both turned out to have causes worth recording.

**Usernames are now stored uppercase, not merely displayed that way.** §11m had uppercased the top bar in CSS specifically so stored names kept their casing; the owner then asked for the names themselves. That is a data change, so migration 009 is written to refuse rather than guess: `username` is unique *case-sensitively*, so 'Card' and 'card' can both exist today and would collide as 'CARD'. The migration raises with the offending groups and changes nothing, because silently renaming somebody's account is worse than stopping.

One transform, `normalizeUsername`, sits on every write **and every exact-match lookup**. The lookups are the half that is easy to miss: a stored 'CAR' is not found by `.eq('username', 'car')`, so a `?profile=car` link shared before the change, or a friend request typed in lower case, would both silently fail to resolve. It deliberately uses `toUpperCase()` rather than `toLocaleUpperCase()` — the latter is locale-sensitive, and in Turkish locales 'i' becomes 'İ', which is outside the `[A-Za-z0-9_]` pattern the schema CHECK enforces, so a player called "iris" would have been rejected by the database. A test pins that.

The database gets a `check (username = upper(username))` too, because migration 004's column grant lets a player PATCH `username` directly through the REST API — the client is not the last word. Two comparisons had to become case-insensitive as a consequence: "that's already your name" on rename (typing 'car' when you are CAR is not a rename), and the delete-account confirmation (the friction that matters is typing your name deliberately, not matching its case).

**The reveal-order bug was two defects, and the owner's description named one of them exactly.** A discard's replacement reveal ran its flips through `Promise.all` with a fixed 90ms stagger, while the opening deal ran them in a serialized `for` loop. Rare cards have deliberately slower flips (up to 1200ms vs 180ms), so in the parallel version a slow rare card and a fast common one landed in whatever order their durations produced. The deal path had been right all along; only the discard path was parallel. It is now the same serialized loop, so cards always land strictly left to right.

The second defect is the one the owner identified: "the slower animation starts when it flips the card back over to the back". The rarity-paced duration was being applied to the turn-to-**back** as well as the reveal — but that stage happens while the *discarded* card is still on screen and says nothing about what is coming, so the drama was landing on the card being thrown away. `flipReplaceCard` now takes a separate `backDuration` (always the common speed) and an `anticipationMs` that waits on the face-down back instead of delaying the start, so a rare slot no longer sits showing its old card longer than its neighbours either.

**"The words don't look centered in their little circles" was caused by the uppercasing.** Uppercase text has no descenders, so centring it by its line box leaves the unused descender space sitting below the glyphs and the text reads as riding high — which is why the bar only started looking wrong once §11m uppercased it. Fixed by centring on the ink instead, via a `--descender-nudge` of asymmetric padding. Verified with canvas `TextMetrics` (`actualBoundingBoxAscent`/`Descent`) rather than by eye: a `Range` rect includes both the full line box and the trailing letter-space, so it cannot see either problem. A second, smaller cause on the help button: `letter-spacing` adds dead width after the *final* character only, which pushes a lone "?" left of centre — and the rule meant to switch it off was losing, because `#site-header button` (1-0-1) outranks a bare `.header-help` (0-1-0).

Scores span 300 to hundreds of millions, so the flexible track has to be the rightmost one — then all the variation is absorbed *after* the hand rather than before it. A test measures the actual `left` of every hand and requires one distinct value.

**The score now wears its own rarity** instead of a pill beside it, reusing the very same `--grade-a/-b/-glow` properties the tier classes already define — promoted to a shared `.grade-pill` so the board and the profile cannot disagree about what a rarity looks like. The tier *name* would have been lost with the pill, so it moves to a `title` and an `aria-label`: colour is the at-a-glance signal, but it is not one a screen reader can convey.

**Animated paints, and animated rarity highlights.** Three new paints (Ember rising vertically, Aurora drifting slowly, Prism at level 30) join the two that already moved. Each sweeps a repeating gradient whose first and last stops match — that is what makes the loop seamless rather than jolting once per cycle. Only the top score tiers animate their highlight: a board of 25 simultaneously shimmering rows would be worse than a still one, and motion everywhere signals nothing. Reduced motion slows these to 24s rather than switching them off, since motion is the entire point of the tier. `animated: true` in the registry is picker metadata only — nothing branches on it; it just lets a chip show a ✨, which a still swatch cannot convey.

**The profile's name was "out a little" because a generic element selector reached it.** `.profile-header` is a `<header>`, and the global `header { text-align: center }` written for the board's day/modifier heading was centring the inline-flex nameplate inside its `h2`. Now explicitly left. Under the name sits a best-run plaque: the hand, its category, and the points wearing their rarity. It uses `bestRun` (the highest-*scoring* run) rather than `bestHandLabel` (the highest-*ranked* hand, which can be a different day) — showing one run's cards above another run's points would be quietly wrong in a way nobody would catch.

**The friends checkbox became a Global/Friends toggle**, inline to the right of the board tabs (owner request). Worth the change beyond placement: "Friends only" named one state and left the other unnamed, so the unticked box never said what it was actually showing. Marked up as a `radiogroup` rather than two `aria-pressed` buttons, because the options are mutually exclusive and pressed-buttons announce as two independent toggles. `align-items: flex-end` on the row keeps it level with the LAST row of tabs once they wrap, and at 375px there is no room inline so it drops to its own row. A test measures that it really is to the right of the tabs and on the same row, since `flex-wrap` could silently move it.

### 11o. Hand Names on the Boards, Two-Phase Discard, and the Mobile Login Fix ✅ built (owner: "add hand name to leaderboard above the hand and make the players name text more bold... change to where discarding cards first turns over all the discarded cards first from left to right, then reveals from left to right... time in timer should be bold... align badge emojis better... login on mobile doesn't save")

**The leaderboard names the hand, and derives it rather than storing it.** Each score row already carries `final_hand`, so the category comes from running `evaluateHand` on those cards at render time — the same "derived, not accumulated" rule the career stats follow. A stored label would be a second copy of something the cards already say, and the only thing a second copy can do is disagree with the first. It costs one evaluation per row on a 25-row board. A test proves it is genuinely evaluated rather than read off a string: a row of three 9s plus a wild has to resolve the joker to read "Four of a Kind", and nothing in the row says that.

Guarded, because `final_hand` is whatever the database holds and not something the page controls: `evaluateHand` *throws* on anything that is not exactly five cards, and one malformed historical row must not blank the entire board. A bad row loses its label and still renders its cards.

The label sits inside the existing `.lb-hand` wrapper rather than getting a grid track of its own — it has to stay glued to the hand it describes, and a separate track would reserve width on the Career board, which has no hand at all. The wrapper became a column, so the `flex-wrap` that let cards wrap at 375px had to move down onto `.lb-hand-cards`; left on the wrapper it would have put the name *beside* the cards instead of above them. `justify-content: flex-end` pins the pair to the bottom of the row box so the **cards**, not the label, stay level with the rank and score.

Name weight is set on `.lb-name .nameplate-name`, not on `.nameplate-name`. The nameplate is shared with the header, the profile and the friends list, all of which sit in their own weighted contexts; this is the leaderboard deciding how loud a name is on the leaderboard.

That was correct but still ran each slot's whole turn-over→hold→reveal sequence end to end before the next slot started, so slot 3 was still showing its old card while slot 1 had already revealed — three separate small events rather than one "these are gone / here is what you got" beat. Now every discarded card turns face-down left to right, and only once they are all backs does the reveal begin, also left to right.

Split at the animation layer rather than by copying the motion: `flipCardToBack` is literally the first half of `flipReplaceCard`, and `flipReplaceCard` already skipped its own turn-to-back when handed an element that was face-down, so the two compose back into the identical animation. §11n's `backDuration` parameter becomes redundant on this path — the turn-over is now *structurally* separate from the reveal rather than merely parameterised to a different speed.

The turn-over pass is deliberately **parallel with a stagger** while the reveal pass stays serialized, which looks like a contradiction of §11n and is not: every turn-to-back runs at the same `BASE_FLIP_MS` regardless of what is coming, and with identical durations start order *is* land order. The reveal pass cannot do this because its durations vary by rarity — which was the whole original bug.

**Badge emojis were measured, not eyeballed, and the problem was not what "alignment" first suggested.** Their horizontal advance is already identical across emoji, so names started at the same x and the badges did form a column. The real defect was vertical and about *size*: at `0.95em` an emoji's drawn ink was ~17px tall against the name's 11px caps and hung a full 3px **below the text baseline**. Uppercase names make that worse — caps have no descenders, so there is nothing beside the emoji to justify ink below the baseline (the same property behind §11n's header-centring bug, showing up a second time). `0.82em` brings the ink close to cap height and a `translateY(-0.1em)` re-centres the remainder on the cap band; sag drops from ~3px to under 1px. The lift is a `transform` so it moves ink without touching layout — nothing reflows. It works on a `<span>` only because `.nameplate-line` is `inline-flex`, which blockifies its children; transforms are ignored on true inline boxes.

**The countdown's clock is bold, its label is not**, which needs the time in its own element. Built once outside the tick rather than re-rendered: this redraws every second, and re-parsing markup at 1Hz to change six digits would rebuild the same two nodes 3,600 times an hour. The tier gets full-strength ink as well as weight — at 0.8rem, weight alone barely separates from the muted label beside it.

**"Login on mobile doesn't save" was not a persistence bug**, and the first fix I shipped for it was aimed at the wrong thing. Nothing in the session-handling code is wrong. Verified against the real SDK rather than from memory (auth-js 2.111.0, loaded from the same CDN URL the site uses): `flowType: "implicit"`, `persistSession: true`, storage is `localStorage`, `detectSessionInUrl: true`.

`implicit` matters, because it rules out the obvious suspect. There is no PKCE `code_verifier` pinned to the requesting browser, so a link opened somewhere else is not *itself* a failure — tokens ride in the URL fragment and whatever browser loads that page writes the session to its own storage. Which leaves exactly one way for a link to "not save": the browser that opens it is not the browser the player returns to. That happens in mail apps with an isolated in-app webview (Gmail on iOS, Outlook mobile) and **cannot be fixed by any link**, because no site can write into another browser's storage. It is also narrower than it first looks — iOS Mail hands off to the default browser, and Android Gmail uses Chrome Custom Tabs, which share Chrome's profile. Both persist fine.

**My first fix was a 6-digit code, and the owner rejected it on product grounds**: a button and a code competing in the same email and the same modal read as two different things to do. That was the right call — the code addressed a case that may not even be the one biting, at the cost of making the common path more confusing. `verifyEmailOtp` is deliberately kept in `state/auth.js` with no caller, documented as the escape hatch, because it is the only answer to the isolated-webview case and re-enabling it is a template line plus an input.

**The change actually worth making was to the link itself: `{{ .TokenHash }}` pointed at our own domain, not `{{ .ConfirmationURL }}`.** That variable is Supabase's `/auth/v1/verify` GET endpoint, which redeems the one-time token the moment it is *requested*. So anything that follows links in an email burns the sign-in before the player ever taps it — Outlook Safe Links, corporate filters, antivirus scanners, prefetchers. The player then gets "invalid or expired" through no fault of their own, which is a far more likely everyday failure than the webview case and is genuinely fixable. A `token_hash` link is inert to all of them, because redeeming it needs JavaScript to run. It also keeps the URL on cardle.lol and lets a dead link say so, instead of dumping the player on a logged-out home page with no explanation.

The callback is awaited in `main.js` *before* anything renders, so an arriving player is simply logged in rather than watching a signed-out header swap a beat later. That is a top-level `await` on a CDN-backed module — the exact shape that once bricked the whole game (§11a) — so it is gated two ways: it only runs when `token_hash` is present, so ordinary loads await nothing, and it cannot throw. A browser test with esm.sh aborted outright asserts the game still loads. The credential is stripped from the URL on both paths, so a reload cannot retry a spent token and it never reaches history or a `Referer` header.

**The likeliest cause is still configuration, not code, and only the owner can check it.** `emailRedirectTo: window.location.origin` is ignored unless that origin is in Auth → URL Configuration → **Redirect URLs**; Supabase then silently falls back to Site URL. A Site URL left at `http://localhost:8080` from setup sends the phone to a host that does not exist there while the laptop works perfectly — which is precisely the reported symptom. `supabase/email-templates/magic-link.html` carries the setup requirements alongside the markup, because a dashboard is not version control and the template is one half of a contract whose other half is `verifySignInLink`.

**The template's own comment had a bug worth recording.** Supabase renders the file through Go's `text/template`, which does **not** skip HTML comments — so naming `.ConfirmationURL` in the explanatory prose would have substituted it, embedding a live single-use sign-in URL inside a comment in every email, exactly where a URL-extracting scanner could burn it. The comment now writes variable names without braces. Caught by a preview script that asserts no `{{ }}` survives substitution, which is the only automated check an email template can really have.

**Verified** with 41 new browser assertions. The reveal-order ones drive the *real* board — deal, mark, lock in — and sample the DOM every 20ms, classifying each discarded slot as showing its original card, its back, or a reveal; the load-bearing assertion is that no frame ever contains both a revealed slot and an un-turned one. A scoring-only modifier is pinned first, because Second Look and Double or Nothing restructure the discard flow and the suite would otherwise pass or fail depending on the date. The sign-in ones cover a good link (verified with `token_hash`, the link's own `type` passed through, URL scrubbed, game rendered underneath), a dead one (login reopened with a reason, token still scrubbed, header still logged out), that no trace of the removed code UI survives in the DOM, and that a blocked esm.sh still yields a playable board.

**One mistake of my own, in the test rather than the product.** The reveal sampler polls at 20ms while `waitForFunction` polls on rAF, so the wait could go true and the interval be cleared before the sampler ever observed the *final* card's reveal — which surfaced as a spurious "not left-to-right" failure caused entirely by the harness. The measurement now gets a settle window before it is read. Worth recording because the failure looked exactly like a real ordering bug.

That last case matters beyond convenience: offering "Add" to someone who has already asked you is precisely how the reciprocal duplicate got created.

Prefix matching rather than substring, because it is what a name picker is expected to do and it is the only form a b-tree index can serve — `like '%x%'` would table-scan every profile on every keystroke. Stored names are uppercase (§11n) and the query is normalized to match, so a plain `like` is both correct and indexable where `ilike` would not be. `%` and `_` are escaped, since `_` is a legal username character that would otherwise act as a wildcard. Input is debounced and each search carries a sequence number, so a slow early response cannot overwrite a later, more specific one — the classic out-of-order autocomplete bug. Result rows are labelled from the friends/pending/sent lists the panel has *already* fetched, which keeps a keystroke to one query instead of one plus a lookup per result.

**Add Friend on a player's profile** (owner request) closes an obvious gap: you could see someone on the leaderboard, open their profile, and then have to go back and retype their name somewhere else. It renders the relationship rather than a fixed label, and only the two actionable states are clickable — removing a friend or cancelling a request stays in the friends panel where the confirmation context is.

**The paint seam was arithmetic, and the old comment asserted the fix without having it.** A percentage `background-position` does not mean "percent of the image": CSS resolves it against (container − image). So for a background-size of *s* box widths, each 100% of position moves the image by (s−1) box widths while one repeating tile is *s* wide. A loop is seamless only when the sweep covers a whole number of tiles:

    sweep × (s − 1) = k × s        →      sweep = 100% × s / (s − 1)

The paints used sizes of 250–320% with a flat 200% sweep, covering 0.2–0.27 of a tile — so every cycle ended part-way through the gradient and snapped back. The stylesheet already claimed seamlessness, but on the strength of matching first and last gradient stops, which is a *different* condition: matching stops make the join invisible in space, and do nothing about the jump in time. Both are needed.

Every horizontal sweep is now `background-size: 200%` with a 200% travel — `s = 2` is the one value where the formula gives a round number, which makes the pairing hard to get subtly wrong. Durations are unchanged, so the paints drift slightly more slowly, which is the right direction for a complaint about smoothness. The same defect affected the `grade-sheen` score pills, which nobody had reported. Ember was already correct (vertical, exactly two tiles) and is untouched.

**Verified** by rendering the loop's first and last frame and comparing bitmaps — the definition of seamless, since the browser snaps back to frame 0 at the end of an iteration, so any difference between the two *is* the jump. 5/5 jumped before, 0/5 after. A suite now asserts this for all eight animated surfaces plus the numeric rule, so a future edit that changes a size without its sweep fails loudly. Plus 28 browser assertions on the social changes, including that a reciprocally-duplicated friendship renders as one person.

**"Poem" is now "Fortune"** everywhere a player can read it. The internal identifiers stay `story*` (`src/story/`, `generateStory`, `STORY_SLOT_ORDER`) — that is the module boundary the feature has always been built on, and renaming a dozen internal symbols to chase a label would be a large diff with nothing to show for it.

**Two mistakes of my own, both in test code, one instructive.** Patching an older suite's fake Supabase client, I emitted `/\(.)/g` — an unmatched paren — into a JS *template literal*, so the fake failed to parse. The dynamic import rejected, `supabase-client.js` swallowed it exactly as designed, and every page silently rendered logged-out with **no error anywhere**; three previously-passing sections started failing for what looked like unrelated reasons. Found by extracting the template and running `node --check` on it, which is now the first thing to try when a fake "does nothing". Second, I misread a Playwright timeout as failing on the click I had just changed when it was actually failing on an earlier `#header-friends-btn` — the log truncates the locator, so the reported failure was not the real one.

### 11q. The Pinned Modifier That Un-Pinned Itself ✅ fixed (owner: "when i switch the modifier of today i think it resets when another version is pushed")

Deploys had nothing to do with it. The override lives in `game_config`, which a deploy never touches — a push was simply the thing that prompted a reload, which is when the reset became visible.

**The real cause was a one-hour disagreement about what day it is.** The modifier rotation is keyed on the GAME day (§11l), which rolls at 19:00 New York. `modifierOverrideFor` was keyed on `toISOString().slice(0,10)` — the UTC calendar date, which rolls at 20:00 ET during EDT and at 19:00 ET during EST. In winter the two are identical, which is why this held together for so long; in summer they disagree between 19:00 and 20:00 ET, every single day.

That window did two separate wrong things. A pin kept applying for an hour *after* its day had ended, leaking yesterday's modifier onto the new hand. And a pin saved *during* that hour was filed under the previous date, so it never applied to the day it was chosen for — the one that reads as "it reset".

**§11l's own note said this had been fully cleaned up, and it had not.** That section records "seven files used to open-code `toISOString().slice(0,10)`; all now call `gameDayFor`". Two were missed: this one and `ui/admin.js`'s schedule builder. Both are exactly the places where being a day out is invisible until it isn't.

**A second, latent bug in the same function.** The admin's 14-day schedule stepped days with `new Date(Date.now() + offset * 86_400_000)`. Adding fixed 24-hour blocks drifts across a DST change, because that shifts the New York wall clock by an hour and can carry an instant over the 19:00 boundary — so a schedule spanning a transition could list a day twice or skip one. Two new helpers fix the class rather than the instance: `addGameDays` steps the LABEL (a date string has no timezone and no DST, so it cannot drift), and `instantWithinGameDay` returns noon UTC, which is 07:00–08:00 in New York — after midnight and well before the reset — so it maps back to the same game day year-round. Midnight UTC, the obvious choice, would be wrong all summer: it is 20:00 the previous evening, already past the reset.

**Verified by reverting the fix and watching the tests fail** — 1 unit test and 2 browser assertions, which is the only way to know a regression test is testing anything. The browser suite fakes the clock rather than waiting for the real boundary, since the defect is only observable for one hour a day. It asserts both halves (a new day's pin is live the instant the day starts; yesterday's does not leak) plus that the admin's "Today" row is keyed to the live game day, and a property test round-trips `instantWithinGameDay` through `gameDayFor` for all 365 days of 2026.

**Two comments were repaired because they were part of the cause.** `deck.js`'s `dailySeed` claimed "every OTHER date-keyed daily rotation derives its own seed the same way" — untrue since §11l, and precisely the kind of confident, stale assertion that gets a UTC convention copied somewhere it doesn't belong. It now carries a warning that it is NOT the game day and has no production callers. `story/generator.js` had the old UTC comment left sitting directly above the corrected one, saying the opposite thing.

  **Bug found and fixed (owner report): "i set the luck slider to 500x and i only get bronze cards, rarely silver gold or diamond."** The original implementation shrank the RAW roll (`roll / luckMultiplier`) before the same cumulative-range lookup real daily deals use — since `RARITIES`' tiers are checked bronze-first and bronze's own cumulative boundary (0.07) is far bigger than any shrunk roll once `luckMultiplier` exceeds ~14× (`1 / 0.07`), the entire shrunk range collapsed to fit inside bronze's slice alone, making bronze the *only* reachable outcome — silver/gold/joker/diamond became mathematically unreachable, and this got *worse*, not better, as luck went up (the opposite of what a luck slider should do). Fixed by rewriting `rarityForRoll(roll, luckMultiplier)` to scale the COMBINED chance of landing any rarity instead (`min(TOTAL_SPECIAL_CHANCE × luckMultiplier, 1)`), then split that total among tiers proportional to their real relative shares (70:30:10:2:1) — the same proportional-renormalization `jokerTierForRoll()` already used for a Joker's own sub-tier. At 500× this correctly saturates to a guaranteed rarity every card (matching the admin panel's own "~100% chance per card" hint text, which had been telling the truth about intent the whole time even though the underlying roll never delivered on it), split roughly 62%/26%/9%/1.8%/0.9% across bronze/silver/gold/joker/diamond. Verified live: 20 redeals at 500× turned up bronze, silver, gold, *and* diamond (joker missed in that particular sample, consistent with its ~1.8% share — a larger sample in the new unit test coverage confirms it's reachable too). 296/296 tests passing (6 new: `rarityForRoll`'s scaling behavior directly, plus an integration-level `dealHand` sample confirming every tier is reachable at high luck).
- **Force buttons** — one per tier (Bronze/Silver/Gold/Diamond) plus one per Joker flavor (also Bronze/Silver/Gold/Diamond, §3h/§3p) — redeal and guarantee that exact rarity (and, for Joker, exact flavor) on one random card slot, independent of the luck slider — the deterministic "show me a Diamond Joker right now" path, for when you don't want to wait on odds at all.
- **🎛️ Custom Hand Builder** (§3r) — 5 independent slot rows, each with its own rank/suit/rarity (and, for Joker, flavor) selects, plus a "Deal This Hand" button that deals exactly that 5-card hand. `buildCustomHand()` sources the draw pile from the rest of the standard deck, shuffled and rarity-rolled with a fresh seed, so post-discard replacements still show real rarities; duplicate rank+suit picks across slots are rejected before dealing.

None of the three touch `dailySeed`/`hashSeed` or persistence — every redeal draws a brand-new random seed (`freshSeed()`), and the day label switches to "admin redeal"/"custom hand builder" wording so it's never mistaken for the reproducible `?test=<seed>` hand the page loaded with.

59. **Server-side game config — admin pass 2 of 2** (§11g). Completes what §11f deliberately left read-only. The decisions worth recording:
    - **core/ was kept pure.** The tempting shortcut was to have `modifiers.js` and `generator.js` read config directly.     - **Prefix, not substring, and `like` not `ilike`.** Only a prefix can use a b-tree index, and since names are stored uppercase and the query is normalized, the case-sensitive operator is both correct and the indexable one.
    - **Debounce plus a sequence number.** Debouncing alone still lets a slow early response overwrite a later, more specific one.
    - **The paint seam was arithmetic, not taste.** Percentage `background-position` resolves against (container − image), so travel per 100% is (s−1) box widths while a tile is s — seamless needs `sweep = 100% × s/(s−1)`. Standardising on `s = 2` makes the two coupled numbers both 200% and hard to get wrong.
    - **A comment can assert a property the code does not have.** The stylesheet claimed the loop was seamless because the gradient's first and last stops matched — a real requirement, but a different one. Matching stops hide the join in SPACE; only the tile arithmetic hides it in TIME.
    - **A fake that fails to PARSE is silent.** A bad regex in a test's fake Supabase client made the dynamic import reject, which `supabase-client.js` swallows by design — so every page rendered logged-out with no error at all, and three unrelated sections "broke". `node --check` on the extracted template found it in seconds; guessing did not.
    - **Playwright truncates the locator in a timeout log.** I spent several minutes fixing the click I had just changed when the failure was an earlier one.
69. **The pinned modifier that un-pinned itself** (§11q).
    - **"It resets when a version is pushed" was not about deploys at all.** The override lives in `game_config`; a push just prompts the reload where the reset becomes visible. Correlation the reporter offers is a clue, not a diagnosis.
    - **The game day and the UTC date agree in winter and disagree for one hour a day in summer.** That is the worst possible failure shape — it works for months, then misbehaves in a narrow window nobody is watching, and only during EDT.
    - **§11l claimed this cleanup was complete and it was not.** Two of the nine `toISOString().slice(0,10)` sites survived. A note saying "all now call `gameDayFor`" is only true until the next one is written; a grep is worth more than the claim.
    - **Adding 86,400,000ms is not "the next day".** Across a DST change it shifts the New York wall clock by an hour and can cross the 19:00 boundary, duplicating or skipping a day. Step the LABEL, which has no timezone.
    - **Midnight UTC is the wrong instant to represent a game day** — in summer it is 20:00 the previous evening, already past the reset. Noon UTC round-trips correctly every day of the year.
    - **Revert the fix and watch the test fail.** A regression test that has never been seen red is an assumption, not a test. This one caught 1 unit and 2 browser assertions.
    - **A stale comment is a live hazard.** `dailySeed`'s claim that the other daily rotations "derive their seed the same way" stopped being true in §11l, and is a plausible route by which the UTC convention got copied into the override lookup.
70. **Four scoring modifiers, a leaderboard modifier, one removal, and friends-panel names** (§4f, §11r).
    - **Held Card is Locked Card inverted, and the visual language has to say so.** Same "mark a slot" shape, opposite meaning — one forbids discarding, the other bribes you not to. Opposite corner, solid ring instead of dashed, and deliberately no dimming or disabled state.
    - **"Did you keep it?" is not answerable from the final hand.** A replacement occupies the same index, so the scoring callback needed `discardIndices`. Added as an optional context bag rather than more positional arguments, so nothing existing had to change.
    - **One parameterised modifier beats twelve near-identical ones.** Hot Hand names a different category each day from the seeded RNG, the shape Suit Bonus already established — twelve entries would have made hand-type days two-thirds of the roster.
    - **Exclude the categories a bonus cannot pay out on.** High Card scores 0 (multiplying it stays 0) and Royal Flush is too rare to land; either would produce a day indistinguishable from having no modifier.
    - **Exact category match, not "or better".** Flush Frenzy spans every flush because Straight and Royal Flush ARE flushes. Hot Hand has no such family, so "Two Pair day" means Two Pair.
    - **A reverse-ranked board CANNOT be built client-side.** The SQL sends the top N by score DESC; reversing those gives the bottom of the top 25, not the true lowest — the rows you need were never sent. Sorting has to precede the limit, which means it has to happen in SQL.
    - **Containment is what made Lowball shippable.** §12 deferred it because achievements, personality, the decision rating and the EV solver all assume higher is better. Flipping only the board's ORDER touches none of them, and still creates the intended tension against career points.
    - **Clamp a per-day rule to the per-day board.** `fetchLeaderboard` refuses `ascending` on the window boards rather than trusting callers — one day's modifier must not reorder scores from days it has nothing to do with.
    - **An async config read can clobber a synchronous user action.** The server override landed a second after a tester picked a preview and silently replaced it. An explicit action now outranks a fetch already in flight.
    - **A browser suite that is not hermetic will lie to you.** These tests were reaching the real Supabase project, where a pinned modifier overwrote the previewed one — which looked exactly like the preview button being broken.
    - **An unstyled anchor is not neutral.** The friends panel had no rule for its name link, so it inherited the UA's hyperlink blue — which is why only UNPAINTED names looked wrong while painted ones were fine. `color: inherit` is the fix; hardcoding white (as literally requested) would have been invisible on the light theme.
71. **Result panel ending, header spacing, and Share** (§11s).
    - **Two code paths rendering "the same" panel will drift.** The live reveal stacked the hand name above the score; the already-played reload collapsed them onto one line. Now identical markup, with the layout test running against BOTH.
    - **A bare element selector reached a component again.** `header { margin-bottom: 2rem }`, written for the board header, was also padding the sticky site bar — 32px of the 44px gap the owner asked to cut. Same shape as §11n's `header { text-align: center }` hitting `.profile-header`.
    - **Moving a button out of a hidden container makes it clickable too early.** Share used to be inside the story block and therefore unclickable until the fortune rendered; moved out, it was visible seconds before its handler existed. Anything relocated out of a deferred container needs its own reveal.
    - **`[hidden]` trap, SEVENTH time** — `.share-row { display: flex }` needed its `[hidden]` guard. Written at the same moment as the `display`, which is the habit that finally stops this recurring.
    - **Reach the state for real when you can.** The already-played assertions come from actually replaying and reloading, so they would catch a regression in the persistence path too, not just in the markup.
    - **A moving control beats no control, but loses to a well-placed static one.** Sticky met both stated constraints and was still rejected: predictability matters more than reachability once a fixed spot exists that is above the fold anyway. 72. **Header XP bar, mobile hamburger, and the XP flight** (§11t).
    - **When a documented rationale blocks a request, look for a bounded proxy.** XP excluded points because they span 274,000x. The score GRADE is nine tiers, so it delivers "more points, more XP" while leaving that reasoning intact — and it matches the grade the player was just shown.
    - **"Derived, not accumulated" has a cost, and the cost lands on whoever displays it.** The header cannot read a level; it must fetch history and recompute. So the fetch is async (the bar appears late rather than blocking the name) and cached per page load (the profile page needs the same rows).
    - **Decide what stays OUT of a mobile menu before building it.** The nameplate and XP bar stay visible because the XP animation needs a target and the bar should be glanceable; a pill flying into a closed hamburger communicates nothing.
    - **One set of buttons, restacked — never a second copy.** Duplicating the nav into the dropdown would mean applying every `hidden` rule twice, and getting that wrong is how the Admin button once showed to everyone.
    - **Let the media query own visibility.** The menu reappears on resize with no listener, because only the open/closed state is a class; the breakpoint decides the rest.
    - **A block element's box is not its text.** Positioning the pill against `.score-total`'s right edge put it at the panel border, because the block is full-width and the digits are centred. A `Range` over the contents measures the ink.
    - **A -50% transform means the coordinate you set is the CENTRE.** Half the element's width has to be added to clear what it is meant to sit beside — and the result clamped so it cannot leave the viewport.
    - **Colour carries meaning that is already spoken for.** The pill was amber at first because `--selected` was to hand; that is the card-selection colour. `--accent` green both matches the request and ties the pill to the bar it flies into.
    - **Order the final flourish after everything that changes its number.** Achievements award XP, so the pill fires after that branch or it shows a figure that is already stale.
    - **A test encoding an owner request still needs re-reading, not just re-passing.** "The nameplate is the last child" guarded against the name shifting per-player. A hamburger that is present at every width does not do that — so the assertion was rewritten to the intent rather than deleted or blindly satisfied.
73. **The bar actually filling, level-ups, and a local test account** (§11u).
    - **Re-rendering a container kills every transition inside it.** `renderAuthSlot()` replaced the fill element, so it was born at its final width with nothing to animate from. Mutate the node that is on screen. The value was correct throughout, which is why this survived a passing test suite.
    - **Assert the MECHANISM, not just the endpoint.** "The bar shows the right percentage" passed the entire time it was broken. "The fill node is never replaced" and "it passes through intermediate widths" are what actually catch it.
    - **A level-up must not look like the bar going backwards.** Crossing a level resets progress to near zero, so fill to full and flash FIRST, then start the new level from empty.
    - **`transition: none` then a width then restoring it needs a forced layout between.** Otherwise the browser coalesces the writes and the rewind never happens.
    - **Reduced motion is not "remove all movement".** The flash and pop go; the bar still fills, because a progress bar that teleports has lost the thing it exists to show.
    - **Two independent gates, and a fabricated identity that grants nothing.** The fake account needs `?test` AND a deliberate flag, and holds no Supabase session — so at worst it lies to one browser. A test proves the flag alone does nothing.
    - **Synthesise fixtures from real inputs, not from the answer.** The fake history is built from run RESULTS so `derivePlayerStats` does its real work; a stated total would have tested nothing. A base-only run is worth exactly 100, which makes any target reachable on the nose.
    - **A dev tool has to actually move.** Test mode never persists, so the fake account was frozen — runs now bank their XP into it, or "watch the bar fill" was untestable, which was the entire request.
    - **"Works with no backend" is not the same condition as "works".** Every fake-account test blocked esm.sh, so Supabase's `onAuthStateChange(INITIAL_SESSION, null)` never fired and never overwrote the fake session — the exact bug the owner then hit. A stub that is REACHABLE and reports nobody signed in is the case that mattered.
    - **Two sources of identity will disagree.** `getSession()` returned the fake session while the subscription said null, and the header simply believed whichever spoke last. Anything faking identity has to intercept every channel that reports it, not just the one that is convenient.
    - **`prefers-reduced-motion` means reduce, not delete.** Swapping the XP flight for a fade-in-place removed the very thing the animation exists to say. Keep the journey, drop the bounce, scale and speed. Judged twice now — the bar's fill made the same call.
    - **Test the accessibility branch, not just the default one.** Nothing exercised `reduce`, so a whole class of users got a broken feature that every suite called passing. Playwright takes `reducedMotion` per context; there is no excuse for not covering it.
74. **Support footer** (§11v).
    - **A branded third-party button is a remote image.** Ad blockers stop it by default, so the "support" link would be an empty box for a good share of visitors. A styled text link always renders, and keeps the project's one-external-dependency rule intact.
    - **`target="_blank"` without `rel="noopener"` hands the opened tab a handle back to yours.** Cheap to get right, invisible when wrong.

75. **Wild becomes a card property, not a rarity tier** (§3x).
    - **Two ideas sharing one field means neither can vary independently.** Wild occupied the rarity slot, so a card could be wild OR rare, never both. Splitting the axis made "Gold Wild" expressible at all.
    - **Raising a thing's frequency means lowering its per-instance payoff.** Wilds went from ~1 hand in 100 to ~1 in 11, so the multiplier came off and a flat bonus replaced it. Frequency and reward are one decision, not two.
    - **Keep the RNG call count identical when changing what a roll MEANS.** Two calls per card before and after, so every seed still deals the same ranks and suits — the change stayed contained to what those cards rolled.
    - **Stored rows outlive the model that wrote them.** Every saved hand records its wild as `rarity: 'joker'`, and the leaderboard re-evaluates those rows to name the hand. One `isWild()` reading both shapes is what stops history silently rewriting itself.
    - **When a category stops being a category, everything keyed off it needs revisiting** — reveal pacing was a lookup by rarity id, and wild was one of them. It now takes the slower of the two sources.
    - **Writing the test caught the copy lying.** The help text claimed a wild pays nothing, which was true of the design I assumed and false of the one chosen. Documentation is an assertion; test it like one.
    - **A new button with a new attribute needs the listener's selector widened.** "Show Wild" had only `data-force-wild` while the handler bound `[data-force]` — it rendered and did nothing.
